### DiskDoubler ✦

**In scope since 2026-08-15** (M12), where every status file
here used to call it detect-only. Three containers — a single compressed file
(`0xABCD0054`), `DDAR`, `DDA2` — and four methods, which is every method the
corpus uses: 0 stored, 6/9 (an 8 KB-block LZSS), 8 (Compact Pro compression in
a DiskDoubler container: the same two stages as `.cpt`, at a 0xfff0 Huffman
block instead of 0x1fff0), 10 (DDn: a 64 KB-window LZSS whose literals, offsets
and lengths are three separately Huffman-coded streams laid out back to back).
Delta-1 preprocessing is applied where the header asks for it, outside the
checksum, which is where the reference puts it.

Peak heap 8 KB–392 KB on Linux and the same on a Mac Plus at 4 MB and 1 MB
(`macextract t --stats`; FORMATS.md, "DiskDoubler and Now Compress on the
Mac"): the 8 KB end is method 3, which is two buffers and no state at all;
method 7 is 10 KB, whose whole state is a 2 KB window; method 1 is 17 KB on
an 11 KB fork, sized from the input like every other `unc_lzw` caller; DDn is
~100 KB, its window sized from the entry up to the format's 64 KB plus a
literal/offset buffer sized from each block's own header; and the 392 KB end
is methods 2 and 5, which pay 1,536 B per distinct context byte (below).
**Measured on the Mac, 2026-08-22:** `synth-dd-m5.dd` touches all 256
contexts and peaks at **405,520 B (396 KB)** on a 68000 — and passes at 4 MB,
at the **1 MB floor**, and on System 7, which is the largest single
allocation this engine has ever asked a Mac Plus for. Methods 3 and 4 are
8,208 B and 9,508 B there, the cheapest in the format. The **400 KB budget**
column is about the corpus and stays true of it: that one fork is the only
thing in this format that would not fit that budget.

**Methods 1 and 7 joined them on 2026-08-20**, when running DiskDoubler
itself showed that both are reachable after all (BLOCKERS.md, *Withdrawn
claims*):

| method | what writes it | specimens | how graded |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, Unix compress | DiskDoubler 3.7, "Method A (fastest)" | `subject-A.dd`, both forks; one more on the 3.1.2 disk | the bytes that went IN, on the emulated Mac; `unar` agrees |
| 7, Stac LZS | the Sigma Designs card DiskDoubler 3.x drove | the two THINK C libraries on the 3.7 disk, 2,226 B each, **and 22 forks on the 3.1.2 disk, up to 97,906 B in 8 chunks** | the fork's own XOR checksum; `unar` agrees byte for byte |
| 2, splay Huffman | DiskDoubler 1.x-3.1.x | **one**: `XCMD.h` on the 3.1.2 disk, 564 → 386 B (2026-08-21) | the fork's own 16-bit sum, and the output is legible 1990 Salient C |
| 3, RLE90 | DiskDoubler 1.x-2.x | **none** | Salient's 1991 expander, on forks this project encoded (below), at run lengths 3-255 and at **2**, the shortest the format expresses |
| 4, StuffIt Huffman | DiskDoubler 1.x-2.x | **none** | the same, on two trees: one 2-11 levels deep and one over all 256 symbols with **a leaf at depth 1** |
| 5, splay Huffman, tree count in the stream | DiskDoubler 1.x-2.x | **none** | the same, at **every count the format allows** — 256, 128, 64 and 32 — and DiskDoubler 4.1.1 agrees on 256 |

**Methods 3, 4 and 5 joined on 2026-08-21, graded without a specimen.**
`tools/vmac/ddencode.py` writes a fork with our own encoder and hands it to
**Salient's own 1991 DiskDoubler** under Mini vMac; all three come back
byte-exact, and 4.1.1 confirms method 5 a second time. What that grades and
what it does not: DECISIONS.md (methods 3, 4 and 5). Three things it
measured that neither reference states —

- **the 0x5a XOR applies to methods 1, 2 and 5 but NOT to 3 and 4**, on
  headers carrying `info1 = 0x2a`, which is the value every real file of that
  era carries. macutils agrees by construction; **XADMaster's case 4 does
  not**, and is marked untested by its author;
- **DiskDoubler 4.1.1 has dropped methods 3 and 4** — it answers *Unknown
  compression method. The file was probably created by a more recent version
  of DiskDoubler* on a 1990 format;
- **method 5's tree-count byte takes four values and no others**, read out
  of Salient's own expander on 2026-08-28 and all four exercised on
  2026-08-29: `CODE` 11 of the 1991
  `DiskDoubler™ App` validates it against **32, 64, 128 and 256** at 0x1dfc,
  turns 0 into 256 at 0x228c, refuses anything else before decoding a bit,
  refuses a count larger than the trees it could allocate, and masks the next
  context with `count - 1` at 0x1fbc — which is `byte % count` for every legal
  value. A 128-tree fork this project wrote came back **byte-exact from that
  expander**; the count of 16 it rejects is an illegal value, not a
  disagreement (DECISIONS.md 2026-08-28).

**What the 1991 expander accepts, measured 2026-08-29 by making our
encoder emit it.** The gap these three methods carried was never the
specimen supply — Salient's expander does not care who wrote the stream,
so every shape `ddencode.py` can be made to write is a shape it will
grade. Twelve forks, one boot:

| asked | answer |
|---|---|
| method 5 at 32 and 64 trees | **byte-exact**; all four legal counts now exercised, where only 256 and 128 ever had been |
| method 4 over all 256 symbols, skewed | **byte-exact**; a leaf at depth 1 and leaves at depth 10, where the older subject's tree is 2-11 |
| method 3 with `ESC 2`, the shortest run | **byte-exact** — no encoder writes it, because at 2 the escape costs what it saves, so nothing had ever graded our shortest run |
| method 3 with `ESC 1`, at offset 0 and 1,800 bytes in | **refused, both times** — so it is the token and not an escape with nothing to repeat |
| a fork whose compressed size EXCEEDS its original | **refused** — the control, using no token the accepted case does not |

The last two are why the control exists. `ESC 2` and `ESC 1` were both
refused on the first run and both forks had GROWN; reading that as "`ESC 2`
is illegal" would have been the Splay16 mistake again — a size check
reported as a disagreement about the codec. Made to shrink, `ESC 2` is
taken and `ESC 1` still is not.

**`ESC 1` cost this engine a divergence.** `dd_rle` passed `strict = 0` to
the shared RLE90 filter, lenient after macutils, and it was **the only one
of six call sites that did** — StuffIt, ARC twice and BinHex twice all
pass 1. Salient's own expander refuses such a fork; the engine now does
too, and all 91 DiskDoubler fixtures stay byte-exact, which is the
evidence that no real fork contains one (BLOCKERS.md; DECISIONS.md
2026-08-29).

**Method 2 joined on 2026-08-21**, when Salient's own 3.1.2 distribution
floppy turned up on WinWorld — older than any DiskDoubler this project had
(BLOCKERS.md). It is an order-1 adaptive code: 256 splay trees, the one in
use chosen by the byte just produced, each starting as the complete binary
tree over 256 leaves and rotating the leaf it just emitted toward the root.
The 0x5a XOR over decoded bytes is method 1's, under the same `info1`/`info2`
condition; the checksum needs no adjustment because the stream carries no
header of its own. DECISIONS.md 2026-08-21 (the Salient disk).

**Methods 2 and 5 peak on the number of distinct context bytes, not a
constant.** A tree is 1,536 B and is built only when its context first
occurs: the method 2 specimen — 60 contexts in 564 bytes — measures
**101,888 B**, and a synthetic method 5 fork touching all 256 measures
**401,408 B** (`macextract t --stats`), which still fits the 597,036 B a 1 MB
Mac Plus leaves the engine (*Resident memory, stack and launch*, `Budget`). Methods 3 and 4 are the cheapest in the format at
**8,192 B** and **9,476 B**, being two buffers and, for 4, one Huffman
table.

Method 1 is `unc_lzw` behind three bytes of its own, with an 0x5a XOR over the
DECODED bytes and the header bytes — not over the code stream — and a sum
adjusted by those three bytes. Method 7 is a 2 KB-window LZSS whose bit stream
AND output are stored complemented, with match lengths unbounded by the
window. Both: DECISIONS.md 2026-08-20.

No DiskDoubler method is declined any more. What is still missing for 3, 4
and 5 is a real specimen — every fork of them this project has seen, it
wrote itself (OPEN_ITEMS.md). **11 forks across 2 fixtures were adjudicated
by neither oracle** — `unar` failed on them and `macunpack` has no DDn at all
— and **all 33 forks of both archives are now byte-exact against two other
implementations.** DiskDoubler's own expander agrees under System 7 outside
the resource header's OS-stamped bytes 16..127
(`tools/vmac/diskdoubler.py --adjudicate`, 2026-08-21), and `unar` agrees
everywhere including that window once handed one fork at a time with the
declared compressed length one byte larger — the off-by-one its DDn reads
past (`tools/run_ddforks.py`, 2026-08-22). Every hash is in the manifests and
checked by `ci.sh`; `oracle-unadjudicated` fell from 24 forks across 12
fixtures to **16 across 10**, and neither DiskDoubler fixture is on that list
any more. DECISIONS.md 2026-08-15, 2026-08-21, 2026-08-22.

**That masking window has independent evidence since 2026-08-21.**
`DiskTester™` 3.7.7 ships DiskDoubler-compressed on the Norton DiskDoubler
Pro 1.1 floppy and UNCOMPRESSED in the Fifth Generation 4.0 StuffIt archive —
same build, two publishers. Our decode of the compressed copy equals the
other over all 36,104 bytes except 46, every one of them inside 16..127
(`tools/vmac/ndd.py --grade`). Until then the window was masked on the
argument that the Mac rewrites it; this is a fork graded against a copy no
DiskDoubler ever touched.

**A `DDA2` entry of type `0x1000` carries no `0xABCD0054` header of its own.**
DiskDoubler Pro writes it **exactly when the data fork is stored AND the file
has no resource fork** — measured across all four cells of compressible x
forked in one archive on 2026-08-21 (`tools/vmac/ndd.py --stored`;
DECISIONS.md, the Symantec release). Size is not a factor: 40 B and 20,000 B
both take the form. A file with a resource fork gets a full header even when
its data fork is stored, which is 45 of the records in the CC0 corpus against
30 of the headerless kind. Consequences: **no `0x1000` record can carry a
resource fork**, so `+87` never describes one; `+86` is the data fork's
checksum (corrupting one payload byte trips it); and `+54` and `+58` are the
creation and modification dates, measured 2026-08-21 by archiving subjects
stamped 1990 and 1995, not assigned.

Read since 2026-08-21; before that, five of the nine DiskDoubler Pro
4.1.1 archives in `ssokolow/diskdoubler-test-files` stopped at their third
entry, and **`unar` 1.10.8 still stops there** (MEASURED): `lsar` fails on
all five with *Archive parsing failed! (Data is corrupted.)*, and reads all
four DiskDoubler 3.7.7 archives fine.

That corpus is CC0 and publishes the **originals** beside the archives, so it
grades against source bytes rather than against another decoder. Extracted
here and compared (`tools/run_ddtf.py`):

| what | count | result |
|---|---|---|
| single-file `0xABCD0054` containers (7 files × 9 methods) | 63 | byte-exact |
| `DDAR` / `DDA2` archives (methods 1, 6, 8, 9, 10 and stored) | 9 | byte-exact |
| self-extractors, plain and prompt-for-folder, and the same BinHexed | 20 | byte-exact |
| **forks compared against the published originals, in all** | **456** | **exact** |

The TIFF in each set has no published original — it was made on the Mac — and
comes out identical from all nine independent compressions, forks included.
115 of these became fixtures in M17, with a pinned URL and a manifest each,

### Now Compress ✧

**In scope since 2026-08-15** (M12). The archive is a flat
entry table nested by a per-directory entry count, then one compressed stream
per file; each stream is a table of blocks, each up to 32 KB of output against
a 32 KB sliding history, in one of five shapes (stored, LZSS, Huffman,
Huffman then LZSS, and a second "new" LZSS with its own length/offset tables).
Peak heap is a flat **128 KB** — history, block and staging buffers, all fixed
by the format — and it extracts fully under a 400 KB budget. Byte-exact on a
real emulated Mac Plus at 4 MB and at the 1 MB floor (2026-08-15).

The reference treats the whole archive as ONE solid stream and cuts entries
out of it by offset; this reader does not, because each file's stream holds
exactly that file's resource fork followed by its data fork. That is what
keeps it to fixed memory (DECISIONS.md 2026-08-15).

**What the two real fixtures reach, measured** by instrumenting the block
loop. `derived-Expand_Now_1.0.1.sea`: three blocks, "new" LZSS twice and
Huffman-only once, zero padding, no offset delta, no stream restart, no length
past a nibble, deepest code 12. `derived-SyntheticReferences2.0.sea`
(1995, found 2026-08-16): a folder, a two-fork file and a 9,949,696-byte
FileMaker database out of 2.9 MB, in two streams of 306 blocks — all "new"
LZSS, and **two stream-restart records**, the dictionary-cache reload no other
real archive here uses. Peak heap on that extraction is 131,072 B, so the flat
128 KB is measured on a 9.9 MB output.

Everything else — the other three shapes, a 17-bit code and both routes into
the offset-delta search — is covered since 2026-08-15 by 13 generated archives
adjudicated by `unar` (TESTING.md), byte-exact there and under qemu-m68k.

**`unar` cannot read the 1995 archive at all**, so four of its forks are
adjudicated by nobody: 129 of its 306 blocks end within 7 bits of their
buffer, and XADMaster's bit reader raises end-of-buffer there (DECISIONS.md
2026-08-16). The spare-bit count is spread almost uniformly over 0..15, which
is what a real encoder writes. Those four forks were adjudicated on
2026-08-16 by the decoder inside the archive — it is self-extracting —
which agrees with us on every one (DECISIONS.md, "The archive that carries its
own oracle").

**The block shapes come from the encoder itself since 2026-08-16.**
`tools/vmac/nowcompress.py` installs Now QuickFiler 5.0.1 under System 7 on an
emulated Mac Plus and drives its Compress and Archive commands, the way
`cptsegment.py` derives a segmented Compact Pro set. What it writes:

| what | block flags | shape |
|---|---|---|
| Compress ▸ Faster | 0x10 | LZSS only |
| Compress ▸ Smaller, text | 0x1f | LZSS only |
| Compress ▸ Smaller, mixed content | 0x3f | Huffman then LZSS |
| Compress ▸ Smaller, incompressible | 0x00 | stored |
| Archive ▸ Smaller (a `.sea`) | 0x100 / 0x140 | stored / new LZSS |

Seven specimens, every one byte-exact through this decoder and through `unar`.
With the 1993 fixture's Huffman-only block that is all five shapes on real
bytes. Two things only the encoder could say: it REFUSES a wholly
incompressible file rather than storing it, so a stored block appears only
inside a file that compresses somewhere else; and a stored block may carry
flag bit 0x100, which no decoder here or in XADMaster examines
(DECISIONS.md 2026-08-16).

### Alysis SuperDisk! ✧✧

**In scope since 2026-08-20**, where every status file
here used to call it the one format the corpus holds that nothing can name.
An archive is eight bytes nobody reads, an optional chain of 68k decompressor
modules, then a record stream: `0x29` opens a folder, `0x2a` closes one,
`0x28` is a file and `0x27` ends the archive. A file's forks follow its own
record inline — **data fork first**, then resource — each a chain of blocks of
at most 32 KB of output, each block's length counting itself, with bit 15
marking a stored block.

| method | name | shape |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | " Faster" | context predictor, bitmap and literals stored |
| 2 | " Tighter" | bitmap Huffman-coded, literals stored |
| 3 | — | bitmap stored, literals Huffman-coded |
| 4 | — | both Huffman-coded |
| 5-8 | — | 1-4 again, over a 16-bit context instead of a 13-bit one |
| 9 | "30% Solution" | Huffman alone, its region starting at the block's LENGTH word (*Method 9's method word*, below) |
| 10 | "SlowFastA" | LZSS: a 32-bit control word per 32 items, matches of `nibble + 2` bytes at `out - offset + 1`, 12-bit offsets with a 16-bit escape (*Method 10*, below) |
| 11 | "SlowFastB" | LZSS, lengths 2-282, offsets 1-49120 |
| 13 | "SlowFastC" | **declined per entry** — its module only COMPRESSES (*Module 13*, below) |
| 20 | "Tennessee" | **not a block method**: its module is an ENCODER, and what it writes declares method 11 (*Module 20*, below) |

#### Method 9's method word

**And the method is the low BYTE of that word, not the word.** Method 9
keeps its Huffman `startbits` in the high byte, so a 16-bit read of a real
method 9 block gives 0x0809 and this engine refused it as unsupported until
2026-08-25 (BLOCKERS.md). Both halves come from the reference dispatching on
`a2@(3)` — CODE 2 @0x6ba — and calling the Huffman routine with `a2`
unchanged for method 9 (@0x712) where the 1..4 family advances past four
bytes first (@0x812).

**Every method 1..9 now has a second implementation, and it runs on Linux.**
Disk Expander 1.2's `Cmpr` resources are one Add-In Module per codec — 68k
with no Toolbox in them — and `tools/sdmodule/` lifts them out of the corpus
fixture and calls them under `qemu-m68k`. Results, in `./ci.sh`:

| what | through both readers |
|---|---|
| corpus blocks (method 11) | 4, byte-exact |
| specimens Alysis's own compressor wrote (methods 1 and 6) | 22, byte-exact |
| synthetic blocks, methods 1-9 | byte-exact, the refill boundary included since 2026-08-28 |
| method 10, against its module and the bytes behind it | equal three ways |
| method 11 blocks written by Alysis's own encoder (module 20) | 5, byte-exact |

**The one disagreement was this engine's, and it was a decode defect.** Where
a Huffman region's neighbour begins when the coded flag bitmap ends *exactly*
on a 32-bit refill: this engine read the literals four bytes early, so every
literal in such a block came back wrong. The two Alysis implementations never
disagreed — `sd_huff_bit` fetches a long only when a bit is asked for and
theirs has already fetched it, so the cursor `sd_huff_end` adds 2 to is four
bytes further on. **Fixed 2026-08-28** against blocks Alysis's own 1991 panel
wrote at 160 and 256 flag bits (`sdcompress.py --boundary`): wrong from byte 0
before, byte-exact after, with 161 and 257 unaffected either way. It reaches
about one block in 32 of any method 2, 4, 6 or 8 fork; the corpus's two
archives use 7 and 11 and were never affected. BLOCKERS.md has what it cost,
DECISIONS.md how the subject was sized.

**The single-file container joined on 2026-08-22.** SuperDisk! writes two
shapes and this engine now reads both: the record-stream archive, and what
its control panel's "Make File" produces — a 30-byte header, the name as a
Pascal string padded to an even offset, then the same block chains, data fork
first. It stands to the archive as DiskDoubler's `0xABCD0054` does to `DDAR`,
and the payload needed no new codec. Derived from five specimens written by
Alysis's own 1991 control panel under Mini vMac, chosen to separate the
fields — one with a resource fork, one stamped 1990/1995, one with a
20-character name, one over 32 KB — and **all five decode byte-exact against
the bytes that went in**, dates, type, creator and Finder flags included
(`tools/vmac/sdcompress.py --specimens`, then `--verify`; DECISIONS.md
2026-08-22, later still).

**Measured 2026-08-25, and it dissolved half of an open item.** Module

### BinHex: multipart, in both directions ††

**Since 2026-08-15.** A `.hqx` file may hold
more than one document, and a single document may have been posted in several
parts — each part carrying its own mail header, so that joining them puts that
header in the MIDDLE of the stream. The second case was added later the same
day, after the first was: `macextract x posted.hqx.001` used to report the entry
truncated, because the `:` in `From:` is BinHex's terminator. The rule that
fixes it is `reference/macutils/hexbin/hqx.c`'s — judge each LINE and skip one
holding "intermediate garbage" — and it works because the BinHex alphabet omits
`n`, `o`, `s`, `t`, `u` and the space, so ordinary prose disqualifies itself
within a few characters. Half of hexbin's rule is deliberately left out (the
equal-length test, which can discard a legitimate short final line — hexbin
ships `-c` to disable it); what that half would have caught, a junk line built
only of alphabet characters, the per-fork CRC catches instead, as a per-entry
error rather than as silence. Both halves are asserted in
`tools/run_multihqx.py`, and both fail against a build with the rule removed.

**A `.hqx` file may hold more than one document, and since 2026-08-15 all
of them are extracted** — the shape comp.binaries.mac produced, several files
posted in one message. Each document is banner, ':', stream, ':'; the decoder
looks for the next banner after each one, as
`reference/macutils/hexbin/hexbin.c` does. A banner with nothing usable after
it is an error rather than a quiet stop. Graded by `hexbin` and by the same
fixtures decoded one at a time (TESTING.md, "Several BinHex documents in one
file"); the differential suite cannot see this case, because `unar` takes the
first document too. The 179 fixtures are single-document files and are
unaffected — all still byte-exact.

### StuffIt 5: the Arsenic block size †

**Every real Arsenic block is 512 KB.** The block size is `1 << (v+9)`
with `v` read from the stream, so other sizes are legal, but across the
31-fixture StuffIt 5 corpus **all 484 Arsenic streams — one per compressed
fork — declare 512 KB** (`v` = 10, blockbits 19). Measured directly by
instrumenting the point where `sit_arsenic.c` reads `v`, not inferred.

Peak heap is modelled by

    peak  =  ~35 KB parser state  +  block capacity  +  4 × actual block fill

because only the *block* is allocated at its declared size; the inverse-BWT
transform vector is sized to how much of the block the entry actually fills
(`transform = numbytes * 4`, in `ars_bwt_prepare`). Peak heap therefore cannot
recover the declared block size — the earlier "128 KB (14 fixtures) / 256 KB
(9) / 512 KB (8)" figures were inferred by dividing peak by five and are
withdrawn (BLOCKERS.md, *Withdrawn claims*).

Measured peak heap over the corpus (`macextract t --stats`): **min
0.15 MB / median 0.59 MB / mean 0.92 MB / max 2.53 MB**, the maximum
being the entries that genuinely fill a 512 KB block (35 KB + 512 KB +
2048 KB) — **exactly two fixtures reach 524,257 B of fill**, which is the
number that got applied to three fixtures it did not describe (BLOCKERS.md,
*Withdrawn claims*). A true figure about the worst case is not a figure about
the case in front of you. Those are the peaks with memory to spare, where the decoder takes
the fast inverse BWT; under a cap it takes a cheaper one and the numbers
below apply instead.

**Under a cap, since 2026-08-07: 23 of the 31 fixtures extract completely
and byte-exact under a 400 KB budget (was 11), and all 31 fit the ~700 KB a
1 MB Mac Plus has free (was 18).** The transform vector is no longer built
at all when it does not fit; a sampled occurrence index answers the same
query for a fraction of the memory and more time (DECISIONS.md 2026-08-07).
**Peak heap under a budget is not the requirement** — the decoder spends
whatever the cap allows, so the honest figure is the minimum at which a
fixture still extracts fully, bisected:

| fixture | needed before | minimum now |
|---|---|---|
| `derived-RWAsm_Folder.sit` | 160,704 | 71,996 |
| `derived-Troi-ClipSave-Plug-in1.1.sit` | 660,200 | 167,455 |
| `derived-a2basic.dmg.sit` | 1,031,463 | 245,897 |
| `derived-Library_ISBN_Barcoder.sit` | 1,682,080 | 393,028 |
| `derived-Domino_v1.4.sit` | 2,657,700 | 565,062 |
| `derived-EasyFind.dmg.sit` | 2,657,700 | 565,062 |

Those figures are what the corpus costs today. **Arsenic reached the 1 MB floor
in four steps, and the per-fixture outcomes are the two Mini vMac sections
below:** 2026-08-02 the shell stopped slurping the archive, 2026-08-03 the
block stopped being allocated at its declared 512 KB, 2026-08-06 its first
allocation was sized from the entry, 2026-08-07 the transform vector became
optional. Before the first, peak was min 0.63 / median 0.97 / mean 1.23 MB with
**every** entry over a 400 KB budget; nothing about the format or the decoder
changed, only what is allocated to hold it. One DECISIONS.md entry per step,
same dates.

### Unix compress: the `.Z` peak §

**It used to be a flat 274,432 B for every input, and is now sized from the
input — twice.** MEASURED 2026-08-03 and again **2026-08-30**. `compress(1)`
writes `maxbits=16` whatever it compressed, so a 488-byte `.Z` declared a
65,536-entry table it filled a few hundred entries of. The first bound was
`symbols <= 256 + reserved + insize * 8 / 9` (DECISIONS.md 2026-08-03); the
second walks the CODE-WIDTH SCHEDULE instead of assuming every code is the
minimum 9 bits, which is worth a third of the table on inputs big enough to
widen (DECISIONS.md 2026-08-30, *a byte buys fewer symbols the further in it
sits*):

| fixture | in | flat | 8/9 bound | width schedule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `synth-exo-Z.Z` | 488 B | 274,432 B | 15,044 B | 15,044 B |
| `synth-exo-tar-Z.tar.Z` | 5,465 B | 274,432 B | 32,740 B | 29,373 B |
| `a2ps.V4.3.tar.Z` | 30,255 B | 274,432 B | 120,880 B | **86,080 B** |
| `popper-1.7.tar.Z` | 59,323 B | 274,432 B | 224,232 B | **147,796 B** |

The last column is the on-Mac peak, and the last row is the point: 224,232 B
did not fit on a 512 KB Macintosh and 147,796 B does, so that fixture went
from a refusal to a byte-exact extraction without the machine changing

On-Mac figures agree with Linux to a constant 32 B of shell overhead. See
DECISIONS.md for why the table is sized once rather than grown.

### Zip: the peak's lost floor ¶

**The zip peak lost its 66,000 B floor on 2026-08-09.** Every zip used
to allocate the whole span an end-of-central-directory record could
occupy and scan it in memory; it is now scanned backwards through a
515-byte window on the stack, which is what a segmented set needed
anyway — a 66,000-byte read from the end of one spans two parts and
costs a disk swap to satisfy. The peak is now the 12,288 B decode
buffer, plus a deflate window where there is one, plus the central
directory, which is read once so that walking it does not interleave
with reading entry data:

| fixture | was | now |
|---|---|---|
| `4acalc.zip` | 12,288 | 12,493 |
| `4alnch11.zip` | 41,702 | 12,554 |
| `derived-MUSFMT10.ZIP` | 53,892 | 53,892 |
| `synth-exo-zip-deflate.zip` | 52,216 | 52,551 |
| `synth-exo-zip-store.zip` | 12,288 | 12,623 |

A large directory can therefore cost more than it used to on a small
archive — a few hundred bytes here, and unbounded in principle at 46
bytes plus a name per entry. That is why the directory buffer is
optional: when the allocation fails, the reader goes back to fetching
one record at a time and produces the same bytes more slowly, which
`ci.sh` checks by aiming a budget one byte under the run's own peak
(TESTING.md). The bound is the caller's budget, never a constant chosen
in the decoder.

### uuencode: a document posted in several parts ★★

**A uu document posted in several parts** joins the same way, and the test
is stronger than BinHex's: a uu line's first character DECLARES how many bytes
it encodes, so a line whose length disagrees cannot be data and is stepped
over. What uuencode cannot do is notice a part that never arrived — it carries
no length and no checksum anywhere, so a set missing its MIDDLE part decodes to
a short file with nothing to object to (a missing LAST part is still caught:
the terminator and `end` live there). That limitation is asserted rather than
described, in `tools/run_uu.py`; OPEN_ITEMS.md.

### ARC and uuencode ★

**In scope since 2026-08-15** (M13), when the brief
became "everything StuffIt Expander 4.0.2 expands". Neither occurs in this
corpus, so every archive under test is generated and handed to a second
implementation to grade — `unar` for ARC, `hexbin` for the UUTool shape
(TESTING.md). The methods: 1/2 stored, 3 RLE90, 4 squeeze (a Huffman tree of
int16LE node pairs, then RLE90), 5/6/7 crunch (12-bit LZW whose table is
addressed **by hash rather than by sequence**), 8 (12-bit compress LZW +
RLE90) and 9 (13-bit compress LZW), the last two over the shared `unc_lzw.c`;
CRC-16 per entry. **0x0a (Crushed) and 0x0b (Distilled), added 2026-08-25**,

until that day (BLOCKERS.md). Crushed is LZW over 8,192 symbols with a
usage-count replacement policy and a code framing that switches, unannounced,
on how many of the last 500 symbols were strings; Distilled is LZ77 with a
stored prefix code, a fixed 64-entry offset code, an offset width that grows
with the output so far, and an 8 KB window **pre-filled with spaces**.

squeeze 5,124 B, the compress methods 6,660–6,664 B, crunch **32,768 B** —
a 4096-entry table at 6 B an entry plus a 4 KB stack — Distilled 14,764 B,
Trimmed 20,628 B, and Crushed **46,520 B**, the format's ceiling: five
8,192-entry arrays plus a 500-byte window. uuencode is a flat 4,096 B, whatever the file holds,
because it decodes a line at a time.

#### Method 0x0a is two methods

**Which one an archive means is the
ARCHIVE's property** (2026-08-25). PAK calls it Crushed; ARC 7.x — "ARC
Plus", SEA's last version — calls it **Trimmed** and means LHarc's `-lh1-`
coder with a stop code, match lengths one shorter (3..59), a window of zeros
rather than `-lh1-`'s preload, and RLE90 after it. `src/unc_lzah.c` therefore
serves three formats: StuffIt method 5, LZH `-lh1-` and this.

`arc_dialect()` decides once per archive, on deark's two signals: an
informational item (0x14/0x15) or a subdirectory (0x1e), which ARC 6/7 write
and PAK does not; and PAK's extended records after the end-of-archive marker,
which start `0xfe` and a byte no greater than 4. **Trimmed only when the first
is present and the second is not** — where neither appears the answer is
Crushed, because PAK 1.0 wrote neither and its archives are the ones in this
corpus. A wrong answer costs the entry its CRC; it cannot pass quietly.

**The ARC 7 container had to be read before any of that mattered.** Every
archive ARC+Plus writes opens with a 0x14 item whose name field is empty and
whose uncompressed length is 0, which this walk rejected as corrupt — and
`unar` still does, so **XADMaster cannot open an ARC 7 archive at all**. The
items are skipped; what they carry is a version string ("ARC 7.12 by SEA,
Inc.") and per-file notes.

**How these three methods are graded, since none has an oracle in the
ordinary sense.** Five ways, and each covers what the ones before it cannot:

| grader | covers | reaches |
|---|---|---|
| the CRC-16 PAK stored per member | every member of every archive, real or generated | 27 members: 6 Crushed, 21 Distilled |
| the bytes that went in | the generated corpus only | 7 archives, `tools/pak.py` |
| deark (0x0b) and `unar` (0x0a) | byte-for-byte, independently implemented | 13 members of the two real specimens, all 7 generated archives |
| PAK 2.51 itself, extracting under DOSBox | the period-accurate case: the writer reads it back | the 9 members of the PAK distribution, byte-identical |
| a plain copy of the same file from a different CD | the case with no decoder in it at all | `XARC.EXE`, 21,174 B, Trimmed in ARC+Plus's own distribution and uncompressed in `swsii/072/XARC.ZIP` — byte-identical |

`unar` is **not** consulted about 0x0b, cannot be consulted about Trimmed
(it does not open the container), and deark cannot be consulted about
Crushed: the first is wrong about that method and the second does not implement
this one (BLOCKERS.md). The two real specimens are `arc2pak1.pak` (3 members,
0x0a, 1988) and `derived-INTRECHO.pak` (10 members, 0x0b); both are in the
differential suite, the second with six of its ten entries adjudicated by
deark because `unar` fails them.

**What the real specimens do NOT reach, and the generated corpus does.**
Neither real archive fills Crushed's 8,192-symbol table, so neither reaches
the replacement scan or the second code framing. `pak-crushed-big.pak`
(300 KB, one member) fires the replacement scan **49,751 times** and the
same-length-code mode **13,009 times**. On the Distilled side, matches into
the space-filled window — the case that separates a correct decoder from
XADMaster's — occur 10 times in `derived-INTRECHO.pak` and twice in
`pak-distilled-text.pak`, which is what makes both worth keeping.

### ArcMac ★★★

**ArcMac** — the Macintosh container, `0x1b` then the ordinary ARC header
59 bytes in — **extracts both forks since 2026-08-16**. One compressed stream
holds them, **data fork first**, with the CRC over the concatenation; the
header's two fork lengths are **little-endian**, as ARCMAC.DOC's *ArcMac File
Structure* states and both real archives confirm. Neither fact is in `arc.h`,
which is what this decoder was transcribed from, and the order is in no
document at all — it was measured by having ArcMac 1.3c archive a two-fork
file under Mini vMac (`tools/vmac/arcmac.py`; DECISIONS.md). Two archives in
the wild now read end to end: `ARCMACRC.BIN` (6 entries, methods 8 and 9, 1988)
and `ArcPop.Arc`. Splitting the stream across two forks allocates nothing, so
peak heap stays the underlying method's: `Packed.ARC` (method 8, 4,230 + 333)
extracts **byte-exact on a real emulated Mac Plus at 4 MB**, 1 file, 0 errors,
**8,000 B peak**, both fork CRCs equal to the bytes that went into ArcMac —
encoded on an emulated Mac, decoded on one. `unar` reads none of these: it
cannot recognise the format at all, which is why the encoder had to be the
oracle.

## StuffIt SpaceSaver

**In scope since 2026-08-22**, read out of Aladdin's own engine rather than
from a specimen — there is no specimen of any kind, and the layout came
anyway. `tools/vmac/spacesaver.py --layout` prints every figure below out
of the installed software's own bytes, with nine witnesses that fail if a
different build is ever put in front of it; DECISIONS.md (2026-08-22, the
layout / the codecs) is how it was read.

The writer is `SSEn 1` in `StuffIt Engine™`, not the control panel: the
panel's `ACMP 1` only reads eight bytes and compares the two magics. A
compressed file carries type `SPSV`, creator `SIT!`, an EMPTY resource fork,
and a data fork whose length is rounded up to a multiple of 256 — which is the
screen the system extension applies before it opens anything.

| off | size | field |
|---|---|---|
| +0 | 4 | `'SIT!'` |
| +4 | 4 | `'DFD3'` |
| +8 | 4 | version, 3; 1 and 2 are converted in place on read |
| +12 | 4 | header length, 84 |
| +16 | 4 | flags; `0x00000041` in everything this engine writes |
| +20 | 16 | fork record — data fork |
| +36 | 16 | fork record — resource fork |
| +52 | 4 | the file's `fdType` before compression |
| +56 | 4 | its `fdCreator` |
| +60 | 2 | its `fdFlags`; six bits are cleared on compress and restored from here |
| +62 | 2 | zero |
| +64 | 20 | reserved, zeroed on every write |

| off | size | fork record field |
|---|---|---|
| +0 | 4 | compressed length |
| +4 | 4 | original length; 0 = fork absent, and the two bytes below are 0 |
| +8 | 4 | offset of the stream from the start of the data fork |
| +12 | 1 | parameter set — indexes the engine's `PrAm` resources; always 0 here |
| +13 | 1 | compressor index, bit 7 = the PowerPC module |
| +14 | 2 | zero |

**The records are fixed and the streams are not.** Record 1 is always the data
fork and record 2 the resource fork, but the LARGER fork's stream is written
first, at offset 84, and the other follows it — read each at its own offset.

| idx | modules (`CMod`) | runs on | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 "Default Compressor" | any | stored, implemented |
| 1 | 3 "Original Fast Compressor" | any | **implemented** — LZSS, 12-bit distance* |
| 2 | 4 "Original Slow Compressor" | any | **implemented** — a 4,096-entry position table* |
| 3 | 10 "Alg 3", 11 decompress-only, 15 PowerPC | 68030 and up / any / PowerPC | **implemented** — Huffman + LZ77* |
| 4 | 20 "Alg 4", 21 decompress-only | 68000-68020 / any | implemented |

A file whose compressor byte is none of these is still recognised and named —
the entry fails, the file does not. The index is the module's own byte at
+0x20, not its resource id; the low byte there is a revision and the higher
one wins; +0x24 is a CPU mask, bit 31 − Gestalt `cput`. **The default is the CPU's**: index 4 below a 68030,
index 3 from a 68030 up, and the decompress-only modules accept every CPU so
any Mac can read any file. That was also why 1, 2 and 3 were declined, and **it stopped being true on
2026-08-25**: * `tools/ssmodule/` runs the modules themselves under
`qemu-m68k`, compressor as well as expander, so every method the format has
writes and reads on demand — 71 round trips through Aladdin's own code, in 3
seconds (TESTING.md). **All three were implemented the same day** on the
strength of it, and 44 forks Aladdin's compressors wrote read back
byte-exact here — so every method this format has is now read, and the
per-entry refusal is left for a compressor byte no version of SpaceSaver
ever wrote.

**Methods 1 and 2 share a framing and nothing else.** Both read an 8-byte
block header — a first byte that says "stored" and a 32-bit big-endian
payload length — and loop until the fork's declared length is produced.
Both spend 16 flag bits at a time, LSB first, one per item, and both encode
a match in two bytes: `(B1 & 0x0F) + 3` bytes long, with `B1`'s high nibble
and `B2` making twelve more bits. What those twelve bits MEAN is where they
part:

| | method 1, `CMod 3` | method 2, `CMod 4` |
|---|---|---|
| the twelve bits | a distance | an index into a 4,096-entry table of positions |
| the table | — | 512 buckets of 8, filled by a 3-byte hash of the output, one rotating slot shared by the whole table |
| an entry never filled | — | copies `CMod 4`'s own last 18 bytes, a copyright string, and real streams DO reach it |
| the flag word | a counter, 16 items | a sentinel bit above the word, tested once per item |
| window | 4 KB is enough (12-bit distance) | 64 KB: the table's positions are anywhere in the block |
| peak heap | 4 KB + 4 KB I/O | 64 KB + 16 KB table + 4 KB I/O |

**Method 3 is a Huffman LZ77, and its shape is deflate-like without being
deflate.** The framing is method 4's — a 16-bit block count, a width byte,
then blocks of a 6-byte header — and inside each block:

| | |
|---|---|
| alphabets | 308 literal/length symbols and `4 * wbits + 3` distances, both LSB-first |
| a match | `4 + length`, from base tables the module's init COMPUTES: length extra `i<4 ? 0 : (i-4)>>2` from 0, distance extra `i<3 ? 0 : (i-3)>>2` from 1 |
| the code lengths | a **recursive meta-coder**: a control byte gives a 2–5 bit raw width, an added constant, an escape meaning "length 0", and a bit saying the symbols are themselves Huffman-coded — by the same routine on its own alphabet. The top symbol repeats the previous length `3 + next` times |
| the code assignment | canonical, bit-reversed, **in the order a non-stable quicksort leaves the lengths in** (`CMod 11` @0x15a) — so a decoder has to reproduce that sort, which this one does iteratively because the engine forbids recursion |
| peak heap | 32 KB window + ~5 KB of tables + 4 KB I/O = **41 KB** on a 120 KB fork |

Measured on that fork, the five methods cost **4 KB, 8 KB, 84 KB, 41 KB and
36 KB** and compress it to 120,000 / 81,140 / 67,412 / 48,636 / 66,936 bytes
— Alg 3 is the best of them, which is why a 68030 selects it.

Neither uses the preset dictionary — `CMod 3` and `CMod 4` never read the
parameter block past +52 (measured) — and method 3 needs it as much as
method 4 does: with a null pointer that module faults outright.

**Method 4 is an LZSS with a preset dictionary.** Three bytes of stream header
(block count as a 16-bit little-endian value, then the window's width in
bits), then that many blocks of a 6-byte header — compressed length including
those six bytes but not the three above them, then uncompressed length, both
24-bit little-endian — and a bit stream read LSB-first: `1` and eight bits is
a literal, `0` and five bits of length plus *wbits* of distance is a copy of
4 + length bytes. The window slides across the whole fork.

**Every one of those fields is confirmed by Aladdin's ENCODER as well as its
decoder** — `CMod 20`'s compressor, a different routine in a different module
(DECISIONS.md 2026-08-23, the encoder agrees). Two figures come from it: the
encoder always writes **15** as the width, so a real file's window is 32,768
bytes, and a block covers **65,536 bytes of output** (`ceil(ulen / 65536)` of
them). The decoder reads the width byte rather than assuming either.

**It starts loaded with 9,679 bytes of Aladdin's `PrAm` -4048**, placed
immediately before the first output byte, so the first matches in every fork
read that table rather than anything in the file. **Those bytes ship here**,
in `src/ss_dict.h` — they are Aladdin's, `NOTICE` says so, and DECISIONS.md
2026-08-23 records whose decision it was and why. Without exactly them a file
decodes to output of the right length that is silently wrong, so there is no
approximating it and no generating an equivalent.

The record's byte at +12 chooses: 0 is -4048 and 1 is `PrAm` -4047, four
spaces, which nothing this engine writes selects. Both are built in; anything
else is refused per entry. `macextract --preset FILE` overrides the choice, for
a table this build does not carry. The resource is byte-identical in the
control panel and in `StuffIt Engine™`, so either file will do as a source for
`tools/gen_ss_dict.py`.

**Every method runs on a 68000**, which is the part no Linux test can show.
`tools/run_spacesaver.py --emit` writes three files and `tools/vmac/run.py
--expect` replays them on an emulated Mac Plus under System 6.0.8: a stored
one (**1,040 B data + 512 B resource, peak 4,112 B**), a method 4 one at the
window width SpaceSaver writes (**14,524 B out, peak 36,896 B** — the 32 KB
window plus the 4 KB input buffer), and one whose stream reaches into the
dictionary, which the application expands with the built-in table
(**7,000 B out, peak 36,896 B**). All three agree with the Linux CLI byte for
byte, **at 4 MB and at the 1 MB floor target alike** (re-measured 2026-08-23,
after the width was corrected from the ceiling and again after the dictionary
was built in).

**Methods 1, 2 and 3 joined them on 2026-08-25**, the day they were
implemented: `tools/ssmodule/run_ssmodule.py --keep` writes one specimen per
method out of Aladdin's own compressors and `run.py --expect` replays those
too. All three extract the same 76,800-byte fork byte-exact, **3/3 at 4 MB
and 3/3 at the 1 MB floor**, and the peaks are what the Linux CLI predicted
to the kilobyte:

| method | peak on the Mac | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 "Original Fast" | **8,224 B** | a 4 KB window and the 4 KB input buffer |
| 2 "Original Slow" | **86,064 B** | a 64 KB window, a 16 KB position table, the input buffer |
| 3 "Alg 3" | **42,112 B** | a 32 KB window, ~5 KB of Huffman tables, the input buffer |

**So a lone floppy is enough.** A SpaceSaver file that has travelled without
the machine that made it needs nothing else — not a copy of SpaceSaver, not a
flag — on Linux or on the Macintosh application, which cannot be handed a
dictionary at all. That is the case this format turns up in: the file looks
normal, its own application reads 84 bytes of header where the document
should be, and nothing else on the disk says why.

## LZH / LHA

✵ **In scope since 2026-08-23** (M28), and detect-only until then
because nothing here could grade a decoder: the
Macintosh corpus contains no `.lzh` at all (measured; DECISIONS.md
2026-08-14). What changed is the specimens. `reference/lhasa` ships
**182 archives written by fourteen different LHA implementations** —
MacLHA 2.24, LHa for Amiga/OS-2/OS-9/OSK/X68000, LHarc 1.13, LArc 3.33,
LHMelt, LHArk, UNLHA32 — and `reference/lha` is LHa for UNIX 1.14i,
the original, which this project can compile and run as an ENCODER.

**Three graders, and they are not interchangeable** (TESTING.md):
`unar` is XADMaster, which this decoder is transcribed from, so
agreement with it establishes only that the copy was faithful; lhasa is
an independent reading; LHa 1.14i writes archives whose plain text is
already known. 137 archives are graded against `unar`, 142 against
lhasa, 12 generated sets against their own input.

**What the independent graders were worth**, in the first afternoon:

| what | `unar` | lhasa | settled by |
|---|---|---|---|
| the 18 bytes of an -lz5- window the preload does not cover | zeros | (rotated: zeros) | LArc 3.33's own `initial.lzs`, an archive whose CONTENTS are the initial window — zeros. LHa 1.14i's `decode()` space-fills a dictionary its `decode_start_lz5` never touches |
| an -lzs- window before anything is written | zeros | spaces | the stored CRC: **spaces**. Zero-filling fails `lzs.lzs` and passes `long.lzs`, which is what a difference that only shows on some files looks like |
| LHa for OS-9/68k's level 2 header, two bytes short | fails all three of its archives | compensates by OS byte | lhasa, followed here |
| a 1.2 MB -lh7- file | "Attempted to read more data than was available" | decodes | lhasa, byte-identical to this engine |
| LHArk 0.4d's "-lh7-", which is a different algorithm | fails | decodes it as `-lk7-` | refused per entry here: LHArk marks itself with a space as the OS byte |
| `-lzs-` at all | **cannot open one** — its recogniser omits `-lzs-` while its own parser has a decoder for it | reads it | this probe accepts `-lzs-`; the divergence from the oracle is deliberate and measured |

**MacLHA writes a MacBinary stream and compresses that**, so a Macintosh
entry's decoded bytes are a 128-byte header, the data fork padded to
128, then the resource fork — and the entry's name, type, creator and
dates are the Mac header's, not the LZH header's. The unwrap happens
for OS byte `'m'` and no other: a level 0 header has no OS byte at all,
and `unar` reads MacLHA's own level 0 archives as one 18,304-byte file
where the level 1 copy of the same file is 18,092 plus metadata. Both
readings are reproduced here. lhasa infers the same trigger from the
same byte and then throws the resource fork away; this engine keeps it.

**And MacLHA 2.00 needed one more rule than either reference has.**
None of the sixteen MacLHA archives in lhasa's collection holds a file
with a resource fork, so the split had never met a byte MacLHA wrote —
until `tools/vmac/maclha.py` ran **MacLHA 2.00 itself** under Mini vMac
and archived a two-fork file. Its MacBinary header has **both dates
zero**, which XADMaster's MacBinary-I heuristic refuses (it requires
them non-zero) and lhasa refuses too (it wants the modification date to
match the LZH header's), so `unar` and lhasa both hand back the whole
2,304-byte stream with the resource fork buried in it. This engine
accepts it on arithmetic instead: the name in the Mac header must be
the entry's, and the two fork lengths padded to 128 plus the header must
come to exactly the uncompressed size. Both forks then come back
byte-exact — 1,760 B and 333 B, graded against the bytes that went into
the emulator (`tools/run_lzh.py`, which skips the check where no
specimen has been made). The rule fires on nothing else: all 182
archives and all 8 fixtures are unchanged.

**Dates are not converted.** Levels 0 and 1 carry an MS-DOS timestamp
and levels 2 and 3 a Unix one; neither is a Mac date, and this engine
does what zip's reader does and reports none. A MacBinary payload's
dates ARE used, because those are already Mac dates.

**Symbolic links are reported, not created.** LHA stores one as a single
entry named `link|target`; classic Mac OS has no symbolic links, so the
entry is named and refused per entry — the answer tar's reader gives a
link.

**Peak heap 4–74 KB**, measured with `macextract t --stats`: 4 KB for a
stored entry, 8 KB for LArc, 16–18 KB for -lh5- and -lh1-, 41 KB for
-lh6-, and **74,128 B** for a -lh7- entry big enough to need the whole
64 KB window (`lh7_long.lzh`, 1.2 MB). The window is sized from the
entry rather than from the method, the way StuffIt method 13's is, so
the -lh7- fixtures in the corpus cost 41 KB rather than 74.

**`-lh2-` reads since 2026-08-25, and the encoder for it is this
project's.** Both codes are adaptive there — the literal/length tree
`-lh1-` also uses, plus a position tree that GROWS with the output, one
leaf per 64 bytes, so an offset only becomes expressible once enough has
been produced. Transcribed from `XADLZHOldHandles.m`
(`LHAdecode_start_dyn`, `_c_dyn`, `_p_dyn` and the tree they share),
cross-read against LHa's own `dhuf.c`, in `src/unc_lzdyn.c`.

Nothing writes one, which is what kept it out: lhasa has no decoder,
the LHa in `reference/` carries the coder without selecting it, and even
the maintained fork — which decodes it properly — cannot write one. So
`tools/build_lha_lh2.sh` teaches that fork to, and the archives it makes
are verified by its own decoder before this engine sees them. **15
`-lh2-` entries across three header levels in `ci.sh`**, byte-exact,
with the run failing if fewer than three entries of an archive really
used the method (TESTING.md).

**`-lh3-` reads since 2026-08-25 too**, and it needed no patch to LHa:
its code is STATIC, rebuilt per block, so `tools/run_lzh.py` writes the
streams itself and LHa's own decoder vets each archive before this engine
sees one. A block is a 16-bit symbol count, a literal/length tree sent as
one 4-bit length per symbol, then either a position tree in the same shape
or the fixed one the format keeps; two escapes carry a block that holds a
single symbol and a position tree sent explicitly, and both are exercised.
Six entries per run.

PMarc (`-pm0-`/`-pm1-`/`-pm2-`), `-lhx-` and the self-extracting archives
remain out of scope (OPEN_ITEMS.md, part 1).

**On the Mac** (2026-08-23), five `.lzh` fixtures byte-exact on System
6.0.8 at 4 MB, at the **1 MB floor** and on System 7.0.1: peak heap
4,112 B for the smallest, 41,364 B for the -lh7- one, 16,788 B for the
five-entry Amiga archive — the Linux figures plus the shell's constant
32 B, at every memory size.

## btoa, the other ASCII armour

**In scope since 2026-08-23**, and it came out of a menu: StuffIt Deluxe
3.5.1's Translate menu is built from the files in
`System Folder:Extensions:Aladdin:Translators`, and there are fourteen
of them. Thirteen are formats this project already reads —
`AppleSingle`, `BinHex4`, `CompactPro`, `DeArc` (ARC), `MacBinary`,
`Text`, `UUCode`, `UnPack` (PackIt: its own `STR#` 129 holds `.pit`),
`UnZip`, `Unix Compress`, `tar`, and Segmenting and PKG below. The
fourteenth was `btoa/atob`, and nothing here had heard of it.

Paul Rutter's btoa is uuencode's rival from the Unix side: four bytes
become five printable characters in base 85, which is 25% overhead
against uuencode's 33%. A Mac met it the same way it met uuencode —
over the wire from a Unix host — and Aladdin shipped a translator for
it, which is what puts it inside this project's brief ("everything
StuffIt Expander 4.0.2 expands", DECISIONS.md 2026-08-15).

◆ **Two framings, and they differ in more than the header.** `xbtoa5`
lines carry a checksum character of their own and the three
whole-stream checksums cover the ENCODED characters; the older `xbtoa
Begin` has neither the per-line check nor the same coverage — its
checksums are over the DECODED bytes. Both are read. `z` stands for
four zero bytes and, in version 5 only, `y` for four spaces. The
declared length is what ends the file: the encoder always writes whole
five-character groups, so `(len - 1) & 3` says how many bytes of the
last group are real.

**CR, LF and CRLF all end a line.** The Unix encoder writes LF, but the
machine this decoder is for writes CR and nothing else, so a file that
reached a Mac and was saved there comes back CR-terminated; for CRLF the
LF that follows reads as a blank line, which the format allows anywhere.
Skipping CR instead — which is what the code did until 2026-08-23 — turns
such a file into a single line that no header matches (*BLOCKERS.md*,
withdrawn claims).

**No corpus file, and no second reader.** `xbtoa` appears in none of
the 903 fixtures, `unar` does not recognise the format and macutils
never had it — so the grading is the original implementation itself.
`tools/fetch_references.sh` fetches btoa 5.2.1 (Paul Rutter, Joe Orost
and Stefan Parmark, 1996, from `archive.debian.org`), `src/fmt_btoa.c`
is transcribed from its `atob.c`, and `tools/run_btoa.py` compiles the
same source as an ENCODER and requires our decoder to reproduce what it
writes — eight subjects across both framings, plus a mail-wrapped one,
a flipped character, a truncated archive and prose that merely contains
the word.

One thing it inherits from the Unix side: the checksum called `R` is a
32-bit rotate that the reference computes in a `long`, so a 64-bit host
prints sixteen hex digits where a Mac prints eight. The low 32 bits are
the same either way — the rotate tests bit 31 in both — and that is
what is compared. **Both widths are now measured rather than reasoned**:
the Macintosh specimen below ends `R fcd84997` where this machine's
reference writes `R 273efff1fcd84997` for the same payload.

**A stream written by Aladdin, 2026-08-26.** Every other btoa file this
project has ever seen came out of `reference/btoa` compiled here, so a
decoder that only understood what that binary emits would have passed
everything. `tools/vmac/sitcrypt.py --btoa-send` has StuffIt Deluxe
3.5.1's own `btoa/atob Translator` encode a subject on an emulated Mac
Plus (*tools/vmac/README.md*), and what came back:

- It writes the **older `xbtoa Begin` framing**, not `xbtoa5` — the half
  of the decoder the Unix reference reaches only with `-o`.
- It **MacBinary-wraps first**: `Subject` becomes `Subject.MBin.ascii`,
  640 bytes of MacBinary inside 732 bytes of armour. The subject came
  back byte-exact through both layers — one layer at a time until
  2026-08-27, when btoa and uuencode joined the envelope list above and
  the chain opened in one pass.
- Against btoa 5.2.1 encoding the same payload with `-o`, the two
  streams are **identical but for the width of `R`** — twelve lines, one
  difference, and it is the 64-bit `long` above.

## base64 and MIME, the armour that replaced the others

**In scope since 2026-08-26** (M36), on request — and an open item since
2026-08-25, raised from the Macintosh Garden forum thread *Archive formats*,
which lists `.b64` and `.mime` among what a downloader actually meets. base64 is what
uuencode, BinHex and btoa all became once mail grew MIME: three bytes
into four characters, 33% overhead, and — unlike every armour before it
— a container standard around it that says what the bytes ARE.

Three shapes are read, and one of them is the reason this matters on a
Macintosh:

- **A bare base64 body**, the whole file. No marker of any kind, so the
  probe leans on the shape: four or more full lines of one width
  between 40 and 76 characters, every character in the alphabet, and a
  last line that closes the stream. It is the LAST probe `unc_sniff`
  runs. ◇ **A file too short to have that shape cannot be recognised**
  — four characters on one line is not evidence of anything — and
  `tools/run_mime.py` asserts exactly that rather than leaving it to be
  found.
- **`begin-base64 <mode> <name>` … `====`**, which is what sharutils'
  `base64` and `uuencode -m` write. This one carries a name, and
  several documents in one file are read, as uuencode's and BinHex's
  are.
- **A MIME message**: `multipart/*` to four levels, `base64`,
  `quoted-printable` and unencoded parts, the filename taken from
  `Content-Disposition` first and `Content-Type` second. A `text/…`
  part with no filename is the note the sender typed and is not written
  out — which is munpack's rule, not an invention here.

**A file pasted into a part comes out — since 2026-08-29.** Mail carried
files inside its text before it carried attachments, and a message can
hold both. A `text/…` part with no filename that turns out to be a
uuencoded document is decoded and named from its `begin` line, which is
mpack's own `handleUuencode()` test applied at every depth rather than
only at the top; `Content-Transfer-Encoding: x-uuencode` changes
nothing, because neither implementation knows that value and the body
decides. A BinHex or btoa document in the same position is written out
as it lies, undecoded, for its own extractor to read. Until that date a
message like this yielded ONLY the pasted file and the attachments
beside it were dropped — the sniff order gave the whole message to the
uuencode walk (DECISIONS.md 2026-08-29, *a part that hid its message*).
`unc_sniff` now claims a message that declares a multipart with a
boundary before it runs the uuencode, btoa and BinHex probes; a posting
with no boundary still belongs to those.

**multipart/appledouble (RFC 1740) is the point.** An
`application/applefile` part in front of the data part carries the
resource fork, the real name and the Finder info, and the two come out
as ONE file with both forks and its type and creator — the same thing
MacBinary does for a file on disk. A message whose appledouble has no
data part at all is a resource-only file, which on a Macintosh is most
of them, and it extracts as one.

**No recursion, so no boundary stack on the C stack either.** mpack
recurses through nested multiparts; this walks them with an explicit
stack of four boundaries, and the whole walker — 2 KB of it — lives in
the same heap block as its buffers. The deepest chain the format adds
is under 1 KB (`unc_mime_extract` → `mime_emit` → `mime_dec_run` →
`mime_dec_next`), against a 4 KB budget; the probe's own frame is
904 B, and it is a leaf.

◇ **Peak heap is flat: 5,097 B for base64 and 8,322 B for MIME, on a
4 MB message as on a 40-byte one** (`macextract t --stats`). Nothing
here is sized from the input — the buffers are an I/O window, a line, a
header and the 512-byte prefix of an applefile part — which is the same
reason uuencode's 4 KB is flat. A constant in this matrix is usually a
declared ceiling nobody questioned (CLAUDE.md); this one is a decoder
that never allocates from a field.

**On the Macintosh, 2026-08-26.** A multipart/appledouble message
carrying both forks extracts on an emulated Mac Plus (68000, System
6.0.8) at 4 MB **and at the 1 MB floor**, identical to the Linux CLI on
every field of the result grid — one file, no errors, data CRC
0xB0C0DF2A over 768 B and resource CRC 0x76D73227 over 340 B — with
peak heap **8,288 B** there against 8,322 B on Linux, the difference
being four-byte pointers inside the walker. There is no oracle manifest
for a generated file, so `run.py` reports FAIL and the grid is the
result, the same way SuperDisk!'s two archives are read
(`tools/vmac/README.md`).

**No corpus file.** The sniff sweep claims none of the 940 fixture
files, so every generated case is graded by two other implementations —
munpack 1.6 and deark (TESTING.md, *base64 and MIME*).

**A message a Macintosh wrote — 2026-08-29, 18/18.** mpack's own
Macintosh port is in `reference/mpack`, so the specimen was made rather
than waited for: `encode_applefile()` and `encode()` compiled unmodified
for a 68000 and run on System 6.0.8, writing a 1,912-byte
multipart/appledouble from a real HFS file with a 407-byte resource fork
(`tools/vmac/macmime.py`). Everything the Macintosh half decides is as
this engine reads it — magic `00051607`, version `00020000`, five
entries in macfile.c's order (rsrc, name, comment, dates, Finder info),
the name entry `Subject`, the Finder-info entry 32 bytes of
`TEXTttxt` + flags `0x0100`, and the resource-fork entry equal to the
fork byte for byte. `macextract` returns both forks byte-exact and
munpack reads the same file.

**A message split across several FILES** — `message/partial` — joins
since 2026-08-29 and is the sixth entry under *Segmented archives*
above, not a shape of this reader: the parts are files, so the host
opens them and the joiner sequences them, and what the MIME walk sees
is one message.

◇ **It does not settle line endings**, and nothing about the format
could: the specimen is LF-terminated because Retro68's newlib does not
do the `\n`→CR translation THINK C's text mode did, so the ending is a
property of the C library the port was compiled against.
`tools/run_mime.py` grades CR, CRLF and LF separately, which is where
that question lives.

## Encryption

**In scope since 2026-08-23.** Seven ciphers across five formats, and one
thing to know before the table: **nothing in the corpus is encrypted.**
Measured, not assumed — `lsar -j` over all 903 fixtures reports
`XADIsEncrypted` 0 for every entry — so every archive that exercises
any of this is generated by `tools/synth_crypt.py`,
`tools/synth_cpt.py`, `zip -P` or `arc g<password>`, and graded by
`unar -p` where a second implementation exists. TESTING.md, *the
decryption suite*.

| format | cipher | key from the password | verified before decoding? | graded by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| StuffIt classic | an eight-byte feedback XOR, keyed through **StuffItDES** (a DES with its own key schedule and no initial permutation) | iterated DES over the password — **ceil(len/8) folds, measured** (below) — then the archive's `'MKey'` resource, then sixteen bytes stored at the end of each fork | **yes** — the 'MKey' block checks against itself | **StuffIt Deluxe 3.5.1 itself**, on three archives it wrote; `unar -p` on the generated ones |
| StuffIt 5 | **RC4** | MD5(password) cut to five bytes, then the fork's own five | **yes** — MD5 of that key against a hash in the archive header | **DropStuff 5.0.1 itself**, on three archives it wrote under Mac OS 9.2 (*StuffIt 5's RC4*, below); `unar -p` |
| PackIt `PMa5` | XOR, key applied **modulo seven** over eight bytes | DES's PC-1 permutation of the first eight password bytes | in effect — the entry header is inside the ciphertext and carries a CRC | `unar -p` |
| PackIt `PMa6` | **DES**, ECB; the operation that DECODES is DES's decrypt | the first eight password bytes | the same | `unar -p` |
| zip | **PKWARE's** three CRC-32-driven keys | the password bytes, folded in | **yes** — a check byte in the twelve-byte header | Info-ZIP wrote the fixture; `unar -p` reads it |
| zip, WinZip **AES** | **AES** in counter mode, 128/192/256, with HMAC-SHA1 over the ciphertext | PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA1, 1000 rounds over a salt in the entry | **yes** — a two-byte verifier the derivation also produces | 7-Zip wrote the fixtures and verifies them; `unar` 1.10.8 cannot open one at all |
| ARC | a repeating XOR of the password over the compressed bytes | the password itself, and `arc` 5.21q upper-cases it first (measured) | **no** — the format has no encryption flag at all | Debian's `arc` wrote the fixture; `unar -p` reads it |
| Compact Pro | a **64-bit CRC** register; keystream is its second byte, stepped on each plaintext byte. The stream opens with **eight bytes that are decrypted and discarded** — a salt, so two entries with one password differ | nine bytes: a length capped at eight, and the password ADDED into eight accumulators in rotation | **no** — its own message says so | **Compact Pro itself**, both directions. See below |

**The formats NOT in that table have no encryption to read**, and that
is measured rather than assumed: a string sweep over the distribution
images this project already keeps — DiskDoubler 3.1.2, 3.7 and Norton
DiskDoubler Pro, Now Utilities 5.0.1, the Alysis control panel and
StuffIt Deluxe's engine — finds no password feature in any of
DiskDoubler, Now Compress, SuperDisk! or SpaceSaver. (The hits on two
of those volumes are System 7's own File Sharing and Chooser.) BinHex,
MacBinary, AppleSingle/Double, uuencode, tar, gzip and `.Z` have
nowhere to put one.

Three properties are worth stating because a user meets them:

- **An encrypted StuffIt classic archive that lost its resource fork
  cannot be decrypted by anything.** `'MKey'` 0 is half the key
  material and it is not in the stream. The entry reports "encrypted"
  rather than asking for a password it could not use.
- **A wrong password is reported, never decoded to rubbish.** Where the
  format verifies, that is immediate; where it does not (ARC, Compact
  Pro), the entry is decoded once writing nothing to find the key and
  again to write it, so a wrong key never reaches the output.
- **A WinZip AES entry is graded by its authentication code, not its
  CRC.** AE-2 stores no CRC at all, so the ten bytes of HMAC-SHA1 after
  the data are the only check — and they are checked. AE-1's CRC is
  checked as well. The 1000-round key derivation is the slow part on a
  68000: seconds, once per entry, before any output appears.
- **An encrypted entry does not stop the walk.** The entries around it
  come out; PackIt, whose encrypted entries do not say where they end,
  resyncs on the next block magic as it always did.

### StuffIt 5's RC4: graded by Aladdin's own writer

**Measured 2026-08-26.** Three archives written by **DropStuff 5.0.1** —
Aladdin's own StuffIt Engine — running on Mac OS 9.2 under QEMU, on the
transfer volume the host reads back (`tools/vmac/os9mouse.py`; TESTING.md,
*the decryption suite*). The specimens live in the cache, not the repository:

| specimen | password | forks | what this engine returns |
|---|---|---|---|
| `enc-pass.sit`, 296 B | `pass` (4) | data | byte-exact |
| `enc-swordfish.sit`, 312 B | `swordfish` (9) | data | byte-exact; a wrong password is refused, not decoded |
| `enc-twofork.sit`, 378 B | `abcdefghijklmnopq` (17) | data **and resource** | **both forks** byte-exact |

`unar -p` returns the same bytes on all three (its resource fork arrives
AppleDouble-wrapped; the payload matches). The lengths are the ones that
matter: at 4, 9 and 17 characters the classic format's fold count differs,
and **StuffIt 5 shows no such length dependence** — its key is MD5 of the
whole password, so the two-fold question the classic key raised does not
arise here. The two-fork specimen is the one that grades the per-fork key.

**The application that writes them is not StuffIt Deluxe.** Deluxe 5.0.1
installs on Mac OS 9.2 and its installer runs, but the Deluxe application
quits at launch with *"an update to this software is required for
compatibility with the improved Mac OS file system"* — it predates HFS+, and
the guest's boot volume is HFS+. DropStuff, installed beside it, runs
unaffected and shares the same engine. See DECISIONS.md 2026-08-26.

### StuffIt classic's archive key: ceil(len/8), and neither reference has it

The password is folded into the archive key eight bytes at a time, and
**how many times** is the one thing the two references disagree about.
It is now measured, on three archives **StuffIt Deluxe 3.5.1 wrote
itself** under Mini vMac (`tools/vmac/sitcrypt.py`):

| password | characters | folds | XADMaster master | `unar` 1.10.8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `password` | 8 | **1** | 2 (the second all zeros) ✗ | 1 ✓ |
| `swordfish` | 9 | **2** | 2 ✓ | 1 ✗ |
| `abcdefghijklmnopq` | 17 | **3** | 2 ✗ | 1 ✗ |

So the rule is `ceil(len / 8)`, and **neither implementation has it**:
XADMaster's loop folds an extra all-zero block at exactly eight and
never more than two blocks however long the password is; `unar` 1.10.8
folds one, whatever the length. Each opens archives the other cannot,
which is exactly what the disagreement looked like from here while
every encrypted `.sit` in the project was one this project had
generated — a generator built from a reading agrees with that reading
at every length.

Under eight characters all three agree, which is why this went
unnoticed: it is the length a person picks when testing.

### Compact Pro's cipher: read out of the application, and graded by it

This is the one format here whose encryption no second implementation
reads — The Unarchiver's format notes say "the algorithm for this is
unknown", CptExpand's manual says it "will not extract encrypted
files", and StuffIt's own Compact Pro translator answers "the file is
password protected". So it was read out of Compact Pro 1.51's `CODE` 3
in both directions (DECISIONS.md 2026-08-23, *decryption*), and
`tools/vmac/cptcrypt.py` had the application grade that reading, the
way Salient's expander graded DiskDoubler methods 3-5.

**Both directions agree** (2026-08-23, `tools/vmac/cptcrypt.py`):

| what | result |
|---|---|
| three archives **Compact Pro encrypted** (RLE-only, data forks of 96, 100 and 103 bytes) | every byte back, through our reader |
| one **Compact Pro encrypted, LZH-compressed, two-fork** archive (196 B resource + 312 B data) | both forks byte-exact — which is also the check that the register runs across the two forks without resetting |
| an archive **we encrypted**, handed to Compact Pro | it extracted all three entries, and its own CRC-32 over both decrypted forks passed |

Encryption is gated behind **registration** — an unregistered copy
answers Extract with *"This version of Compact Pro cannot validate or
extract encrypted files"*, and the Add dialog's Encrypt checkbox sits
at a negative rect until a registration moves it into view. Macintosh
Garden publishes one for 1.51, and with it the gate opens.

The eight-byte preamble is the part no reading of the decrypt loop
alone would have caught: an encrypted entry's stream opens with eight
bytes that the reader decrypts and **discards** (`CODE` 3 @0x1f5e moves
the read cursor past them), and their length is counted in the
compressed size of the first fork that has any bytes — a 400-byte
resource fork is stored as 408. It is a salt: the register steps on
them, so the same password over the same bytes encrypts differently in
two entries. What found it was arithmetic on three archives the
application wrote — 96→104, 100→108, 103→111, a constant +8 and
therefore not a block cipher's padding.

### The archive comment, which Compact Pro calls a Note

Text belonging to the archive rather than to any entry. **Two formats in
scope carry one**, and both are saved rather than read and dropped: the
CLI writes `<archive>.comment.txt` beside the extracted items and prints
it on `l` and `t`, the Mac shell writes a `TEXT`/`ttxt` document called
`Comments` into the destination folder (DECISIONS.md 2026-08-23,
2026-08-26).

| format | where it sits | ceiling | saved since |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compact Pro | one length byte at the top of the index, then MacRoman bytes | 255 | 2026-08-23 |
| Zip | a two-byte length in the end-of-central-directory record, then the bytes, at the very end of the file | 65,535 | 2026-08-26 |

`UNC_COMMENT_MAX` is therefore 65,535 and is **not a buffer size a host
may adopt**; the engine hands over its own. A comment it cannot allocate
for or read is dropped, and the archive still extracts.

Graded three ways, in both directions:

| what | result |
|---|---|
| the **six** corpus Compact Pro archives that carry one — `derived-DigSim_RAM_Circuit.cpt` (*"Hi there."*, 1990) and the five Compact Pro Package installers, whose notes run to 250 bytes over several CR-separated lines, one of them Shift-JIS mislabelled as MacRoman | the saved file and the printed line equal `lsar`'s `XADComment`, in `ci.sh` |
| the **one** corpus zip that carries one, `derived-MUSFMT10.ZIP` — 495 bytes of author, address and format list from 1994, twice what a Note can hold | the same, plus the Mac shell's `Comments` document byte-for-byte against the oracle on x86-64 and under qemu-m68k (`tests/run_mac_sink.sh`) |
| a **two-line note typed into Compact Pro 1.51** under Mini vMac (`cptcrypt.py --make --plain --note`) | 61 bytes back byte-exact, the line break stored as a **CR** exactly where Return was pressed, `lsar` agreeing |

Neither costs peak RAM: the zip comment's allocation is taken and freed
before the extraction buffers, and `derived-MUSFMT10.ZIP` measures the
same 54,856-byte high-water mark with the comment saved as without it.

The CR is why the two hosts differ in one place and only one: the saved
file keeps it, and the line the CLI PRINTS turns it into a newline,
because a CR sent to a terminal overwrites the line just printed.

## AppleLink packages, and why they are named but not opened

**Named since 2026-08-23.** Every status file here used to say `.pkg` had no
reference implementation anywhere reachable and could not even be sniffed
without inventing a magic number. The first half was withdrawn on 2026-08-22
(BLOCKERS.md) — installing StuffIt Deluxe 3.5.1 to chase SpaceSaver put
`Extensions:Aladdin:Translators:PKG Expand Translator` on the disk — and this
is the second half.

**A package is TEXT.** The translator's recogniser (`Othe` 0 @0x2952) pulls
whitespace-delimited tokens out of the file — @0xf18, which skips space, tab
and CR, honours double quotes and treats backslash as an escape inside them —
and accepts the file when the first token is `Decode` and the third is `to` or
`into`, compared with `_CmpString` and so case-insensitive. `STR# 129` is the
rest of the vocabulary: `Decode`, `to`, `into`, `convert`, `noConvert`,
` Folder`, `.pkg`, and the errors *"Destination not found: "* and *"No items
matching: "*.

`macextract sniff` implements that test and nothing more, so it is exactly as
strong as Aladdin's: a text file that happens to begin *"Decode this to that"*
is a package as far as either program can tell. Measured against the corpus —
**928 fixtures, 0 false positives**.

**It may not be an archive at all.** Three measured things point that way:
the identify path resolves its operand with `PBGetCatInfo` (`_HFSDispatch`
selector 9), the errors in `STR# 129` are *"No items matching: "* and
*"Destination not found: "*, and the translator carries recognisers for eight
other formats by extension and type. **Hypothesis:** a package is a transfer
manifest naming files that sit beside it, which would make naming it the whole
capability. DECISIONS.md 2026-08-23 has the evidence and what would settle it.

**Two more things the bytes say.** A package expands into a folder named
after itself — `.pkg` stripped, `" Folder"` appended, so `Report.pkg` becomes
`Report Folder` (@0x2e5c) — and the expansion itself is not in the translator
at all: it opens the file and then calls `GetResource('Eng ', 2)` in
`StuffIt Engine™` and jumps into it (@0x334c). The translator is a driver.

**What Aladdin's own expander asks for is a FOLDER.** Measured 2026-09-01,
seven boots: `Translate ▸ PKG Expand…` puts up a Standard File dialog that
lists **only directories** — the filter proc it hands `_SFPGetFile` tests
`ioFlAttrib` bit 4 (`Othe` 0 @0x215e) — so a package written here cannot be
selected at all, as `TEXT/ttxt`, `PACT/EXTR`, `PACT/ttxt` or `TEXT/EXTR`, with
or without one selected in the application's own window. `CPT Expand…` in the
same boot lists the type-`PACT` files, which is what makes that empty list a
fact about the command rather than about the harness. Its own `DITL 2051`
carries a button *"Expand into selected folder"*, `DITL 2052` is a *"Name of
new folder:"* dialog, and its `Help` resource says the translator extracts
"files and folders from archives created by AppleLink". The other two routes
run a translator and do nothing with a package either: StuffIt Expander 4.0.2
lists one and expands nothing, `DropConvert™` takes one into *Items to
Convert:* and produces nothing — each in a boot where the BinHex control
expanded (DECISIONS.md 2026-09-01, *the package that cannot be handed over*).

**Detect only, and the reason is a specimen.** What a `Decode` line refers to
has not been read out of the expander, and no `.pkg` file has ever reached
this project, so there is nothing to grade a reading against. `tools/vmac/
spacesaver.py --pkg` re-derives everything above out of the translator on the
installed image, with four witnesses that fail if a different build is ever
put in front of it; `--pkgexpand`, `--pkgexpander` and `--pkgconvert` are the
three routes above.

implementation or spec for them is in `reference/`"*, and for lzip that was

**lzip is the weakest probe in this file and is ordered last for it.** Four
ASCII bytes with nothing behind them: a text file that opens with the word
LZIPPER is claimed, which is measured rather than feared. deark rates it 50
against xz's 90 for the same reason, and this engine has no confidence to
report — so it is tested after every corroborated magic, and only a file that
matched nothing else can reach it.

**brotli is not on that list and never will be.** RFC 7932 §9.1 is the entire
stream header — one to seven bits of WBITS, no magic, no signature, no
version byte — so a raw brotli stream cannot be identified from its contents
by this or any other program. That is a property of the format, not a missing
reference, and it moved to OPEN_ITEMS.md part 1 on 2026-08-29.
`reference/notes/brotli_rfc7932.txt` is kept as the evidence for a negative.

A `.zipx` needs nothing: it is a zip, the zip probe claims it, and its entries
are refused one at a time by method. A `.cpgz` is gzip, the gzip layer unwraps
to the cpio inside it, and that cpio is now named too.

**Apple Archive joined on 2026-09-01**, and it is the only one of the fourteen
graded by a writer built for the purpose. libNeoAppleArchive — MIT, and not a
reimplementation of Apple's closed libAppleArchive but a from-scratch reading
of the format — builds here with `tools/build_neoaa.sh` and writes archives in
all four shapes the probe has to recognise, each read back by that same library
before the probe is asked (`tools/neoaa_write.c`). The item that tracked it
read *"unnamed for want of a reference"*; what was missing was the search, not
the reference (BLOCKERS.md). It is also the only probe here whose cost was
refused once by the Macintosh ratchet: 164 B of code, against 20 B of headroom
(*Budget*, and DECISIONS.md 2026-09-01).

## Envelope auto-unwrap

`macextract x` unwraps chained envelopes by content sniffing
(never by extension): MacBinary, BinHex, AppleSingle/Double, gzip, .Z,
base64, btoa and uuencode layers that yield exactly one file are
recursively extracted and the intermediate deleted (`-nr` disables;
depth ≤ 8). MIME is deliberately not on that list (DECISIONS.md
2026-08-26 and 2026-08-27). Verified chains: `.sit.bin`, `.sit.hqx`,
`.tar.Z` (tools/run_unwrap.py, in CI), `Subject.MBin.ascii` and a
uuencoded MacBinary document (tools/run_btoa.py, tools/run_uu.py).

A **StuffIt segment** is an envelope too, and joins that list: the
parts are joined, the file inside comes out, and if that file is an
archive the same recursion continues into it.

## Segmented archives (multi-part sets)

One logical file cut across several, one per floppy — or, for the
sixth, one per mail message. **Six schemes are implemented**: StuffIt
segments, Compact Pro segments, spanned ("split") zip, plain `.NNN`
splits, DiskDoubler's `SPLT` and `message/partial`. They differ in how
much a part can prove about itself, and everything else follows from
that (DECISIONS.md 2026-08-08, 2026-08-09, 2026-08-10, 2026-08-14,
2026-08-21 and 2026-08-29).

### StuffIt segments

XADMaster's "StuffIt split file":
a 100-byte header on each part: `B0 56 00`, part number, the original
file's name, and 26 fingerprint bytes at offset 68 (type, creator,
Finder flags, both dates, both fork lengths). Concatenating the parts'
payloads in order gives the original file — resource fork, then data
fork. `src/fmt_sitseg.c` has the layout and its source.

Two properties of that header decide the design, and DECISIONS.md 2026-08-08
has why they decided it: **identity is content, not name** (a part belongs to a
set if its name bytes and those 26 bytes agree — the `misnamed` case in the
suite joins correctly and `unar` cannot read it), and **the set is
self-terminating** (`rsrclen + datalen` is the exact payload total, so no part
count is stored anywhere and none is needed).

**The join is read strictly forward: 0 backward crossings**, measured
over the 3-part and 12-part cases (`macextract x --stats` reports part
opens and how many were backward). That is what decides the cost on
removable media — a backward crossing is a disk the user has to put
back — so it is measured rather than assumed.

Peak heap for the join is 8 KB: one buffer, no part table beyond the
caller's ~1.1 KB scratch struct, one part open at a time.

**Parts inside envelopes** — `Archive.sit.1.hqx`, which is how a set
was actually distributed — join on the Linux CLI since 2026-08-13. The
envelope comes off each part before the join rather than after it, and
the search is rationed: envelopes beside the archive are opened only
while the set is short of a part, at `UNC_SEG_HEAD` decoded bytes each,
and a part is decoded whole only once its fingerprint places it. Mixed
envelopes, wrapper names that say nothing, and parts in three
directories all join byte-exact; `unar` handed part 1 of such a set
unwraps that part alone and exits 0. **In the Mac shell too since
2026-08-14**, where a decoded part lands on the output volume as
`MacExtract part N` and is deleted when the join is over. Not for the two
name-placed schemes, on Linux or Mac: a wrapped part's filename is the
wrapper's, and no reference says what that should look like
(DECISIONS.md 2026-08-13).

### Compact Pro segments

The one scheme whose layout is derived rather than transcribed: nothing
documents it, so it was measured on sets cut by Compact Pro 1.51 itself
(`tools/vmac/cptsegment.py`, DECISIONS.md 2026-08-14). Every segment
carries the same 8-byte header an ordinary archive has — signature,
volume, a 16-bit set identity shared by the whole set, and an index
offset that is 0 in all but the LAST segment. `src/fmt_cptseg.c` has the
layout and what would falsify it.

**Identity is content, not name** (the author's manual: "the names of the
files are not important"), so discovery matches headers exactly as it
does for StuffIt, and **any** segment opens the set — the application
itself requires the final one. An entry is addressed as (volume, offset
within that volume's file), so this reads in the same addressed mode as
a spanned zip.

Part opens (backward), measured, beside the floor each archive's own
directory permits — `run_segments.py` derives that floor independently:

| case | segments | measured = floor | without the index buffer |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cpt4` (9 files, folders) | 4 | 5 opens, 1 backward | 19 opens, 8 backward |
| `cpt5` (one entry) | 5 | 6, 1 | 6, 1 |

The last column is the fallback when the index allocation is refused:
same bytes, more crossings (DECISIONS.md 2026-08-14). Peak heap ~20 KB,
the ordinary Compact Pro reader's 16 KB of buffers plus the index.

`unar` cannot read one: given the final segment it calls it a whole
archive and writes garbage with exit 0 (MEASURED, and asserted by the
suite).

### Spanned ("split") zip — `NAME.z01` … `NAME.zip`

The parts concatenate with nothing to strip, and the archive addresses
itself as (part, offset within that part): "disk number start" at
offset 34 of each central-directory entry, and the two disk numbers in
the end-of-central-directory record. `src/fmt_zipseg.c` has the layout
and its sources.

Only the FIRST part (a local file header at offset 4) and the LAST (the
EOCD, the only place the part COUNT appears) identify themselves; the
parts between are undistinguished data, and `unar` refuses one too. So
the host orders parts by filename and the engine adjudicates the result
structurally — part count, central-directory position, every entry's
local header, every CRC-32. A deliberately lax reader (CRC and
signature checks removed) fails exactly the suite's *wrong order* and
*foreign part* cases, so those checks are load-bearing.

`zip -s` writes its `50 4B 07 08` marker even when the archive did not
need splitting. Such a file says "disk 0", needs no joining, and reads
as an ordinary zip — it sniffed as *unknown* before 2026-08-09.

The join is **not** read forward — the directory is at the end — but it
costs exactly one backward crossing, which is the floor the format
allows. Part opens (backward), measured, beside the floor each
archive's own directory permits; `run_segments.py` computes that floor
independently and requires the two to be equal, so this cannot drift
(DECISIONS.md 2026-08-09):

| case | parts | measured = floor |
|---|---|---|
| `zstored` | 5 | 6 opens, 1 backward |
| `zdeflate` | 4 | 5, 1 |
| `znested` | 3 | 4, 1 |
| `zmany` | 12 | 14, 2 |

`zmany` needs two because its central directory begins on part 11 and
crosses into part 12; where the directory sits is part of the floor.

Peak heap **12.4–13.0 KB** across those cases: the 12,288 B decode
buffer plus the central directory, which is read once into memory.
When that allocation does not fit the budget the reader falls back to
fetching one directory record at a time — same bytes out, at 16 opens
instead of 6 — so the bound is the allocator's rather than a constant
chosen here. Both paths are exercised in `ci.sh`.

### `message/partial` — a message split across several FILES

**In scope since 2026-08-29.** RFC 2046 section 5.2.2, which is how a
file too big for a mail gateway crossed one: each part is a message of
its own whose body is a slice, and `Content-Type: message/partial`
carries an `id` every part of the set repeats, this part's `number` and
the set's `total`. `src/fmt_mimeseg.c` reads that header;
`reference/mpack/decode.c` `handlePartial()` is the source, and mpack
writes the sets `tools/run_mime.py` grades against munpack.

Two things are unlike every other scheme here:

- **A part's header is its own mail headers, so each part's is a
  different length.** Every other scheme has one header length for the
  set, in the descriptor; this one asks the part (`unc_mimeseg_head`,
  the joiner's `hdr` hook), which re-proves the part's number and id
  while it is in there.
- **The set states a part COUNT and no byte total**, so the joiner
  learns the length when it opens the last part. Nothing in the format
  says how many bytes the message weighs.

**Identity is content, not name**: the parts are matched on the `id`,
so a set whose files are called anything at all joins, and two sets in
one directory do not mix — both asserted. A part is identified by
`unc_segment_describe` rather than `unc_segment_match`, which is the
only scheme that needs the difference: mail headers are longer than the
100 bytes that call is given. `total` is required only on the LAST part,
so a set opened anywhere else asks the parts it found — the same
question a Compact Pro set asks about its final segment.

**What is joined is the part bodies, and nothing else.** mpack composes
a standalone message out of the enclosing headers as well; part 1's body
already begins with the enclosed message's own headers, so the joined
stream is a message and the MIME walk reads it directly — no file is
written in between, and a `multipart/appledouble` split across files
comes out with both forks (DECISIONS.md 2026-08-29, *a message in
several files*).

A missing part is REFUSED, before anything is written: the set counts
its parts, which is what a uuencoded posting missing one cannot do
(OPEN_ITEMS.md, part 1).

**Joined on a Mac Plus, 2026-08-29**, at 4 MB and at the 1 MB floor:
four `message/partial` files named nothing alike, carrying a
`multipart/appledouble`, become one Macintosh file with both forks —
773 B of data and a 406 B resource fork, read back off the Out volume
and compared byte for byte, with the on-screen grid agreeing
(`tools/vmac/segcheck.py --case mimeseg`). The Mac shell needed the same
two additions the CLI did: a candidate identified with
`unc_segment_describe` where 100 bytes cannot decide, and the count
taken from whichever part carries `total`.

### Plain `.NNN` splits

No header, no marker, no part count: the scheme is the naming
convention (`reference/XADMaster/XADSplitFileParser.m`). The host
builds the descriptor, the engine joins the consecutive run `.001` …
`.NNN` flat, and one entry comes out — the joined file, named without
the extension — for the caller's next layer, as for a StuffIt segment.
The joined LENGTH is the sum of the parts', which is the only place it
exists, so every part is located before extraction starts.

Part opens are one per part, 0 backward, on every case (`--stats`,
required to equal that floor in `ci.sh`). Peak heap is the 8 KB
streaming buffer; nothing else is allocated.

**What adjudicates such a set is the file it joins into**, and only
that. Measured, both directions: a swap of two parts of a split zip is
refused by the payload's own offsets and CRCs, unless the swap involves
part 1 — after which the join no longer sniffs as an archive and
nothing objects. A set missing its tail is undetectable in every case.
DECISIONS.md 2026-08-10.

### DiskDoubler `SPLT`

DiskDoubler's own Split command, in scope since 2026-08-21.
`src/fmt_ddseg.c` has the layout, derived from the six specimens in
`ssokolow/diskdoubler-test-files` (four when it was derived — BLOCKERS.md); why each field is read the way it is,
and the rule that a `SPLT` header beats a `.NNN` filename, are
DECISIONS.md 2026-08-21.

A 94-byte header per part, carrying more than any other scheme's: the
set's 26-byte identity, the part INDEX, the part COUNT, both fork
lengths of the original file, and that part's own payload length. So it
is the only scheme here where a part is matched by content AND the set's
size is known — a missing tail is detectable, which the plain `.NNN`
split cannot manage either way — and the only one where bytes appended
to a part never reach the join. It stores no NAME; the joined file takes
the opened part's, minus the `.N`. The payload is the DATA fork then the
resource fork, the opposite of StuffIt's split.

**No reference reads it** (MEASURED, 2026-08-21): `unar` 1.10.8
recognizes neither a part nor the parts concatenated. All three specimen
sets reassemble byte-exact, forks included, against the files they were
cut from — including the 14,128-byte resource fork of the
self-extractor, the largest fork any of them carries.

**And the loop closes without an oracle at all**: DiskDoubler Pro 4.1
splitting a 1,024,000-byte file under Mini vMac
(`tools/vmac/ddpro.py --experiments`) produced a two-part set that this
engine joins back to the bytes that went in, byte for byte — cut on a
Mac, joined here, compared with the input, which is the same bargain the
method 1 specimens make.

Part opens are one per part, 0 backward. Peak heap is the 8 KB streaming
buffer. The CRC-16/XMODEM at offset 48 is exact on all six specimen
parts and is **checked while a part is read straight through**, which is
what joining a set does (DECISIONS.md 2026-08-21).

**The 4 bytes at offset 4 are the Mac's `TickCount` when the file was
split** (MEASURED 2026-08-21, `tools/vmac/ddpro.py --experiments`):
DiskDoubler Pro splitting three files 72 s apart wrote 7,581, 11,905 and
16,235 — differences of 4,324 and 4,330, which is 60.06 and 60.14 per
second against the Mac's 60.15 Hz tick. Two of the three files were
byte-identical with different names, so it is neither a checksum nor a
hash of the name. It is written once per SET, which is exactly why it is
equal across a set's parts and different between sets; the joiner needs
no more than that, and matches on all 26 identity bytes anyway.

## Size limits

**Measured 2026-08-25** on Linux x86-64 (`-O2`), with sparse subjects built
for the purpose. A corrupted copy of the 3 GiB gzip fails as it should, so the
passes are testing something.

| what | limit | past it |
|---|---|---|
| container, any format | **the width of `unc_off_t`**: 4 GiB on the Macintosh, unbounded in the CLI since 2026-08-28 | `UncIO`'s `seek`/`tell`/`size` are `unc_off_t` (`macextract.h`), 32-bit unless a host defines `UNC_OFF64`, which the Linux build now does and the Macintosh build never will — its File Manager takes a signed 32-bit `LONGINT`. A 6 GiB tar of four 1.5 GiB members lists three and exits 1 at 32 bits, four and exits 0 at 64. At 32 bits the CLI says *"larger than 4 GB; this engine addresses the first 4 GB of it"* where it opens the file and hands the engine `UNC_OFF_MAX` rather than a wrapped size (added 2026-08-27; `(uint32_t)st_size` WRAPS, so a 4.5 GB zip was once reported as "not a recognized archive" — a true refusal with a false reason). **Only tar reaches past 4 GiB**: its member positions are a running sum, while zip's directory fields are 32-bit and its Zip64 escape is read as halves |
| declared entry size, tar | **2 GiB** | an explicit guard in `fmt_exo.c`. Since 2026-08-27 a per-ENTRY refusal (`UNC_ERR_TOOLARGE`, listed with `?` for a size): the header states how long the member is, so the walk resumes at the next one. It ended the whole tar until that date |
| declared entry size, zip | 4 GiB − 1 | the format's own field. A 3 GiB stored entry lists and CRC-verifies. A Zip64 extra field can state more; past 4 GiB the entry lists `?` and is refused alone (`UNC_ERR_TOOLARGE`) |
| entry POSITION, zip | 4 GiB | past 2 GB the directory holds `0xFFFFFFFF` and the real offset is in extra field 0x0001, read since 2026-08-27 (below); an offset whose high half is set is refused per entry |
| decoded output, streamed (gzip) | **none found** | 3 GiB and 5 GiB both verify, exit 0. The output size is never held, so nothing bounds it |
| zip entry count | 4,294,967,295 | the Zip64 record's count is read as a `uint32`; a 70,000-entry archive extracts all 70,000 (below) |
| Mac binary | 2 GiB, **not measured** | `SetFPos` takes a signed `long` (`mac/macextract_mac.c`), and HFS caps a fork at 2 GB anyway; exercising it needs real hardware (MANUAL_TESTS.md §3) |

The tar boundary is exact: a 2 GiB − 1024 member lists its size, a 2 GiB one
(0x80000000) lists `?` and fails as an entry. `tools/run_sizelimits.py` holds
both sides and the 4 GB one, in `ci.sh` since 2026-08-27.

**Two formats can express a size the engine cannot address: tar, and a zip
carrying Zip64 extra fields.** Every other format in scope states an entry's
size in a 32-bit field, so no value over-reaches: without a Zip64 field zip's
4 GiB − 1 is the format's own ceiling, and the Macintosh formats inherit HFS's
2 GB. A Zip64 extra field states a size and a position in 64 bits, and one
whose high half is set is refused per ENTRY since 2026-08-27. The other `0x7FFFFFFF` guards in `src/` are a different thing —
probe rejections (SpaceSaver, SuperDisk!, MacBinary: the file is not
recognized, so there is no walk to end) and segmented-set descriptors
(`fmt_sitseg.c`, `fmt_ddseg.c`, where the set describes one file and there is
no other entry to lose). Measured 2026-08-27 on a StuffIt fixture whose second
entry was edited to declare 0xFFFFFFFF, header CRC recomputed: it lists as `?`,
fails alone, and the other eight entries are unaffected.

**A zip's entry count is 16 bits, and until 2026-08-25 exceeding it was
silent.** 70,000 entries listed and extracted 65,535 of them and exited 0;
`unzip` and CPython's `zipfile` both say 70,000. The count now comes from the
Zip64 end-of-central-directory record when there is one, read as `uint32`
halves so that no 64-bit arithmetic enters the engine; a field whose high half
is set is an archive this engine cannot address, and returns
`UNC_ERR_UNSUPPORTED`. Zip64 proper — files actually larger than 4 GB —
remains out of scope (OPEN_ITEMS.md, part 1). `tools/run_ziplarge.py` holds
the boundary from both sides, in `ci.sh` since 2026-08-25; DECISIONS.md
2026-08-25 has why a count is worse than a length.

**The corpus could not have caught it: the largest zip in 932 fixtures has 29
entries**, and the robustness variants perturb a fixture rather than grow one.

**A zip's per-entry Zip64 escape arrives at 2 GB, not 4, and until 2026-08-27
nothing behind it extracted.** A central-directory record whose local-header
offset passes CPython's `ZIP64_LIMIT` (2³¹ − 1) holds `0xFFFFFFFF`, with the
real offset in extra field 0x0001; `fmt_exo.c` read only extra field 0x9901
(WinZip AES), so it seeked to `0xFFFFFFFF`, found no local header and returned
`UNC_ERR_CORRUPT` — per entry, over and over. Measured on a 3.4 GB archive of
23,400 entries written by CPython's `zipfile`: it LISTED all 23,400 (the
directory is intact) and then failed **21,352 of them**, every one at or past
0x80000000, `big/02047.bin` at 0x7FF157D5 passing and `big/02048.bin` at
0x80015800 failing. The same archive now verifies whole, exit 0. The escape is
read as `uint32` halves like the end-of-central-directory record's, so no
64-bit arithmetic enters the engine, and a high half that is set refuses that
entry alone (`UNC_ERR_TOOLARGE`) rather than the archive. `tools/run_ziplarge.py`
holds the boundary in one sparse subject with entries on both sides of it;
BLOCKERS.md has what the wrong ceiling cost, DECISIONS.md 2026-08-27 (*the
escape that arrives at 2 GB*) why the two Zip64 refusals differ.

## Name limits

**Measured 2026-08-27.** A name meets two caps on its way to disk, and
neither is negotiable. The ENGINE carries it in `UncEntryInfo.name`,
`UNC_NAME_MAX` (255) bytes plus a terminator inside a struct that is
**296 bytes on the 68000 target** and travels on a 4 KB call stack; the
FILESYSTEM takes what `pathconf` says, which is 255 bytes per component
and 4,096 per path on ext4 and less on others. Until this date the first
cut names silently — a 457-byte zip path extracted as 255 bytes, exit 0,
under a name that was not the archive's.

| cap | value | what meets it |
|---|---|---|
| `UNC_NAME_MAX` | 255 | every format; the engine's own |
| ustar `prefix` + `/` + `name` | 256 | tar states one byte more than the engine carries |
| uuencode line buffer | 96 | a `begin` line's name is cut here first, before the cap above |
| btoa line buffer | 256 | `xbtoa5 N name Begin` — the name is inside the line |
| one MIME header | 512 | a `Content-Type` `name=` or `Content-Disposition` `filename=` |
| AppleSingle real name | 255 | was 64 until 2026-08-27, which cut a name no other format would have |
| `pathconf(_PC_NAME_MAX)` | 255 on ext4 | one component: 255 MacRoman bytes are up to 765 of UTF-8 |
| `pathconf(_PC_PATH_MAX)` | 4,096 on ext4 | `outdir/name`, and the only one of these that refuses |

**The notice is text, so it is not the filesystem's to constrain.** It
carries the archive's own name IN FULL wherever the format still holds
the bytes when the cut is made — the host passes a buffer in
`UncOptions.long_name` (the CLI: 64 KB; the Mac shell: NULL, which costs
nothing and loses only the text), and `name_truncated` reads 2 rather
than 1 when it was filled:

| where the cut happens | the notice gives |
|---|---|
| zip, directory in memory | the whole name |
| tar (`prefix` + `/` + `name`) | the whole name |
| AppleSingle real name | the whole name, re-read from the file |
| the filesystem, not the engine | the whole name — the engine carried it |
| uuencode `begin` line | the whole name: the line is kept in the host's buffer as it streams past, rather than dropped at 96 bytes |
| a Macintosh folder path that SUMS past the cap (DiskDoubler, Now Compress, SuperDisk!) | the whole path: `unc_path_leaf` writes it into the host's buffer as it descends, since 2026-08-27 |
| LZH, btoa's name field, MIME's applefile name | the length, from the field |
| zip with the directory streamed (budget too small for it), btoa's or MIME's cut LINE | the length where the field states it, and *"the rest was cut before it reached us"* — never the shortened name presented as the archive's |

The CLI's policy is a filesystem's: **shorten and carry on.** The file is
extracted under the shortened name, a component is cut on a UTF-8
character boundary, a name that collides *because* of the shortening is
numbered `~1`, `~2` … rather than refused, and every shortened name is
listed in a notice at the end of the run, with the length the archive
stated where the format knows it. The run still exits 0 — nothing
failed. Only a path past `_PC_PATH_MAX` is refused per entry, because no
shortening of a name makes a path shorter than the directory it goes in.
Reasoning: DECISIONS.md 2026-08-27, *the name cap*.

**The Macintosh application's policy is a filesystem's too, and since
2026-08-29 it says so on screen.** Every component is fitted to 31
bytes, HFS's limit, and **the extension is kept while the stem is cut**:
`LongDocumentName.txt` becomes `LongDocumen.txt`, never
`LongDocumentName.tx`. A collision the cutting creates is numbered
`~1`..`~99` off `dupFNErr`, with the number going **before** the
extension (`dup.txt` → `dup~1.txt`), and a name that still cannot be
made unique fails **its own entry** and nothing else. It reads
`name_truncated` as well, which is how a path the ENGINE cut is reported
by a shell whose own cut did not fire.

Why the suffix survives when 31 bytes are all there is: a Macintosh file
is opened by the type and creator `sink_end` stamps on it, but a file
that arrived WITH a suffix generally came off a machine that has neither
— no resource fork, no Finder types in the archive — so the suffix is
the only thing that says what opens it (DECISIONS.md 2026-08-29).

The archive's own note is in it too: a comment that could not be written
was dropped in silence until 2026-08-29 and is now listed and counted
like anything else that did not come out.

| the report holds | measured |
|---|---|
| lines kept | 64, of which the last **16 are reserved for failures** — 100 renames must not push out the one line to act on |
| text | 6,144 B packed end to end, 1 KB of it likewise reserved. **Names go in whole**: eighteen photographs whose names differ at character 34 are one line repeated eighteen times if the name is cut to a row, and the list exists to say which file became which |
| rows a line takes | as many as the box needs, wrapped at a measured width and **broken after the name** where it has to wrap, so one row is what the archive called it and the next is what became of it |
| rows visible at once | 20, Geneva 9 on 11-point rows, in a 460×300 box at (30,26) — the whole of it on a 512×342 Mac Plus screen. A list longer than that scrolls under the last two, which hold a blank line and the outcome |
| static cost | 6,144 B of lines + 1,632 B of folder memo, against the ~64 KB the 1 MB floor target has spare (*the PAK methods*, 2026-08-25) |
| the application | 184,576 B of MacBinary, from 178,944 B before it |

The outcome — *"Extraction complete."*, and every other sentence the
alert used to carry — is the list's LAST LINE, after a blank one and in
bold, and is pinned to the bottom row whenever the list scrolls, so it
cannot be scrolled out of sight. A run that finished with entries that
failed reads **"Extraction finished."** where the list is shown, and
keeps the old *"Some entries could not be extracted."* where it is not;
a run whose ARCHIVE stopped being readable part way reads **"Extraction
stopped part way through this archive."** — photographed on System 6.0.8
over a `.sit` cut to 60 %, which said *"Extraction complete."* until
2026-08-29 (BLOCKERS.md, *9,320 robustness variants clean*). Where the
list is shown:
with every failure named a row above, a sentence that restates the list
in vaguer terms is worth less than the word that tells this from a clean
run. Then: **Save as Text** writes the same lines,
blank line and outcome as a `TEXT`/`ttxt` document, which is what a
screen with no scrollback needs. A run with nothing per-entry to say
still ends in the one-line alert. Reasoning: DECISIONS.md 2026-08-29,
*the end-of-run report*.

One cap was removed rather than reported: the CLI turned a name into a
filesystem name through a 512-byte buffer, which is short of the 765 an
all-MacRoman name can need, so it is sized from `UNC_NAME_MAX` now and
cannot be what cuts a name.

**The corpus could not have caught any of it: the longest name in 932
fixtures is 120 bytes**, under half the engine's cap, so neither the
differential suite nor 9,320 robustness variants could ever have reached
it. `tools/run_names.py` holds both caps from both sides in zip, tar,
uuencode, AppleSingle and a nine-deep Now Compress tree, in `ci.sh`
since 2026-08-27.

### Launch

**A kilobyte of code is not a kilobyte of launch time; a RELOCATION is.**
Measured by ballast — a block of resident data compiled in behind a temporary
define and referenced from the startup path so `-gc-sections` keeps it — three
runs each on `mv-4M-1x` (authentic 7 MHz), System 6.0.8, booting straight into
the application:

| build | app | relocations | boot |
|---|---|---|---|
| shipping | 150,144 B | 2,376 | **8.9 s** |
| + 64 KB of `const unsigned char[]` | 215,680 B | 2,376 | **8.9 s** |
| + 64 KB as 16,384 pointers | 232,064 B | 18,682 | **10.0 s** |

64 KB that carries no relocations is **below the harness's 0.1 s resolution**;
64 KB that carries 16,306 more is 1.1 s, about **67 µs per relocation**. The
whole application has **2,376**, so everything it pays at launch for its own
size is ≈ **0.16 s of an 8.9 s boot** — the rest is System 6 booting.

That is why the CODE resource is not segmented (BLOCKERS.md, *Withdrawn
claims*), and why moving `ss_dict.h` or the StuffIt tables out of the A5 world
would buy nothing at launch: they are data, so they carry no relocations.

### What the reserve is for, measured at last

**The reserve's job is CONTIGUITY, not volume** — `GetNewDialog` needs one
block, not *n* free bytes — and until 2026-08-31 every figure here measured
volume. That gap is why the reserve was held at 32 KB against a 13,242 B
appetite. **It is now measured.** `-D UNC_AT_CONTIG` samples `MaxBlock()` at
the moment each dialog and alert is created, which is the only moment it
matters:

| run | smallest contiguous block at any dialog | dialogs |
|---|---|---|
| password path, **512 KB** machine (122,808 B free heap) | **118,508 B** | 3 |
| report path, 1 MB | **652,112 B** | 1 |
| segmented set, 1 MB — the mid-run "locate part N" alert | **637,918 B** | 4 |

**Essentially the whole free heap, every time, and the reason is structural:
no dialog is ever created while the engine holds a big allocation.** The
progress dialog goes up before extraction begins; a password or "locate part"
dialog opens BETWEEN entries, after the previous entry's blocks have been
freed. Contiguity was guarding a case this application's shape does not
produce.

**So the reserve is 20 KB**, 1.5x the 13,242 B the shell and Toolbox have
ever been measured using, and the 12,288 B released went to the engine
(*Budget*).

**And what it covers is narrower than it looks.** The progress dialog is
created BEFORE the budget is taken, so it is already out of `FreeMem()` and
the reserve never pays for it; what is left is only what the shell allocates
DURING extraction — File Manager buffers, and a dialog that opens mid-run. On
a heavy 1 MB extraction (`derived-Troi-ClipSave-Plug-in1.1.sit`, engine peak
472,137 B) the shell's share at the tightest moment measured **−33 B**:
nothing beyond the engine, to within the skew between the two samples. The
13,242 B worst case comes from a lighter run where a dialog was up. Verified byte-exact at that size on all four tiers — 69/69 at
1 MB, 66/69 at 512 KB, 69/69 on System 7.0.1, 20/20 on Mac OS 9.2 — and the
password and report dialog phases driven end to end, the password one on the
512 KB machine where the heap is tightest.

**What is still not proven, and it is no longer contiguity:** every figure
above comes from an emulator with a stripped System Folder, no INITs and a
RAM-backed disk. A real Plus with a Chooser extension, a floppy's File
Manager buffers or an AppleShare volume costs more, and that is the volume
question rather than the block question. MANUAL_TESTS.md carries it.

### The Linux budget check under-reads the Mac

**`--budget N` on Linux is necessary and NOT sufficient.** MEASURED
2026-08-30. `derived-XMenu.dmg.sit` needs 566,009 B under a Linux cap and
**16 KB more than that** on a 1 MB Mac Plus; `derived-EasyFind.dmg.sit` needs
the same 566,009 on Linux and **more than 598,932** on the Mac — a gap of over
33 KB. Both refusals are budget refusals (`gRefusedWhy` = 1), not
fragmentation. The Mac allocator charges 16 bytes a block, so a decoder that
allocates often pays a toll the Linux run does not.

**Consequence: "passes `ci.sh`'s budget cap" does not mean "extracts on the
Mac", and the gap is not a constant** — it is 16 KB on one fixture and 33 KB
on another. Every `--budget` figure in this file inherits that caveat. The
only sufficient check is the on-Mac replay with its CRC read-back
(`tools/vmac/run.py`). *This does not contradict the "agree to a constant 32 B"
note under Unix compress:* that is about PEAK HEAP on a fixture that fits,
which is a different quantity from the THRESHOLD at which one stops fitting.

### What the application costs the engine

**Only CODE costs anything. Data, globals and resources are free.** MEASURED
2026-08-30 by ballast on a 1 MB Mac Plus under System 6.0.8, with the ballast
verified present in the binary that actually ran:

| ballast | free heap | budget |
|---|---|---|
| none | 647,096 B | 565,176 B |
| **+11,308 B of code** | 635,768 B | 553,848 B |
| **+6,132 B of `.data`** | 647,096 B | 565,176 B |

Code moves it one for one; data does not move it at all. The reason is the
classic layout: `CODE` resources are handles **inside** the heap `FreeMem()`
reports, while the A5 global world sits outside it. So

    budget = 708,100 - code        (autotest build, 1 MB, System 6.0.8)

which reproduces every figure below. **Three of the four ballast runs measured
nothing** before this came out — twice `--gc-sections` had dropped the ballast,
once it was referenced only from the other `main()` — so check the symbol is in
the binary before believing a null result (`tools/build_mac.sh` records the
method).

**Consequence for the Macintosh shell: put material in resources and tables,
not in C.** A dialog defined in a `DITL` costs the engine nothing; the same
dialog built in code costs its own size on every extraction. `tools/build_mac.sh`
enforces a ratchet on code size for this reason.

Before the two 2026-08-24 changes — Now Compress sized from the fork, and
the adaptive reserve — the same sweep at the then-budget of 130,136 B had
Now Compress at **2 limited of 3 with a peak of zero** (refused whole,
before an entry was read) and StuffIt 5 at 21 limited.

At the roomy System 6 budget of 597,036 B only StuffIt 5 refuses anything
(8 of 31), which is why it is the only format with a memory-for-time path.
**At 130,136 B that picture is quite different**, and it is the reason the
fixed-size windows are worth sizing from the entry after all:

- **Now Compress allocated 131,072 B per ARCHIVE, before an entry was
  read**, and at 130,136 B that failed — so it opened NONE of its three
  fixtures, refused with a high-water of zero. Sized from the largest fork
  (2026-08-24) the small one is 84,248 B, and with the adaptive reserve
  all three open. A per-archive allocation failure is also the one shape
  the engine's contract does not allow: failure is supposed to be per
  ENTRY. Listing now skips the allocation entirely, so `l` works in any
  partition.
- **SuperDisk's window** was a flat 98,304 B and is now bounded by the
  fork: 117,294 → 85,943 B, and `Read Me` in `alysis-disk-expander-12`
  extracts under a 50,000 B budget where it used to be refused.
- **zip's inflate window** was deflate's full 32 KB per entry;
  `synth-exo-zip-deflate.zip` fell 53,515 → 24,843 B. gzip's cannot be
  done at all — its ISIZE is in the trailer, so `fmt_exo.c` passes
  `UNC_SIZE_UNKNOWN`.

**ARC (33,732 B) and SpaceSaver (36 KB measured, 68 KB ceiling) still fit
at 130,136 B**, so they stay on their ceilings — but note that this is a
measurement about today's corpus and today's minimum partition, not a
property of those decoders. ARC's is the awkward one if it is ever needed:
its tables are indexed by a 12-bit code read straight from the stream, so a
smaller allocation changes what a damaged file does, and it needs
`unc_lzw.c`'s two-number treatment.

There is still nothing for a generic adaptive mechanism to work on, and one
could not be graded against this corpus. It becomes real the day a format
with a megabyte-class window arrives, and the Arsenic answer will not simply
port to it — DECISIONS.md (2026-08-03, the LZW table) is the recorded case
of that same fix failing on the second problem. LZH is not that format:
64 KB of window worst case, and it halves.

### Stack

**Measure it; do not assume it.** One command gives every function's frame:

    m68k-apple-macos-gcc -mcpu=68000 -Os -std=c99 -Iinclude -Isrc \
        -fstack-usage -c src/FILE.c -o /dev/null

and the call graph comes from the relocations in the linked objects
(`m68k-apple-macos-objdump -dr`), so the worst path is a sum over the two.

| | before 2026-08-24 | now |
|---|---|---|
| deepest chain in the engine | 6,664 B (`unc_dd_extract` → `dd_fork` → `dd_ddn`) | **3,386 B** |
| Now Compress chain | 5,632 B | ~1,400 B |
| largest single frame | 3,740 B (`dd_fork`) | **2,352 B** (`ars_index_build`) |
| largest frame in the SHELL | — | **1,480 B** (`ExtractOne`) |

#### What the stack actually uses, measured two ways

**The static bound above is not the number the machine needs**, because the
Toolbox and the File Manager run on this stack too and no `-fstack-usage` pass
sees them. `-D UNC_AT_STACK` reports two independent instruments, and it is
worth saying why two: the first one on its own was wrong.

- **SAMPLE** — the stack POINTER, taken in the progress pump, the sink write
  path, the allocator, and inside the Dialog Manager's own filter and
  user-item procs, where the Toolbox's frames are below us. Exact where it is
  taken, blind between.
- **PAINT** — the region between ApplLimit and the stack base, painted at
  launch and read back. Sees everywhere, including inside a Toolbox call, but
  cannot tell a stack write from anything else.

**Swept across seven stack sizes on a 1 MB Mac Plus, 2026-08-31**
(`UNC_STACK_BUMP_KB` rebuilds at any size, so this is a sweep and not a
sample), on `derived-Library_ISBN_Barcoder.sit`, the heaviest fixture there
is:

| stack | SAMPLE | PAINT | floor left untouched |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10,172 B | **5,200** | 5,604 | 4,568 |
| 12,220 B | **5,200** | 6,828 | 5,392 |
| 16,316 B | **5,200** | 9,288 | 7,028 |
| 20,412 B | **5,200** | 15,568 | 4,844 |
| 24,508 B | **5,200** | 18,840 | 5,668 |
| 32,700 B | **5,200** | 25,392 | 7,308 |

**SAMPLE never moves and PAINT tracks the size of the region**, which is what
settles it: a requirement does not change when the thing holding it changes.
Peak heap is 559,805 B at every one of those sizes, so the extraction did the
same work each time.

**Sweeping DOWN is what set the size**, and it is the more useful direction:
PAINT *converges on* SAMPLE as the room runs out — 9,288 → 6,828 → 5,604
against a constant 5,200 — so whatever was helping itself to the spare room
is squeezed out and what remains is the real requirement, **about 5,600 B**.
Through the real UI — Standard File (sampled via `SFPGetFile`'s dialog hook,
which is the Toolbox's own modal loop), the progress dialog and the password
dialog — SAMPLE is **3,134 B** and does not move either, at 10 KB or at
32 KB, and the password dialog drove correctly at every size down to 10 KB.

**So `kStackBump` is 4 KB, a 12 KB stack** — 2.1x the deepest write ever
observed — and **20,464 B** has gone to the engine (*Budget*). Verified
byte-exact at exactly that size on every tier: **69/69 at 1 MB**, 512 KB,
System 7 and Mac OS 9.

**PAINT is not a stack measurement, and this is how that was established.**
Its lowest dirty word sits a near-constant 5–7 KB above ApplLimit at every
stack size. The stack is anchored at the TOP of the region, so if this were
stack use the constant would be its depth from the top; instead the constant
is its offset from ApplLimit. The heap is not the writer either — the
application zone's `bkLim` and `ApplLimit` are the same address, measured, so
the heap stops exactly where the region begins. *Hypothesis, not measured:* a
ROM routine sizing a stack buffer from the space available, which would
explain the anchoring and the tracking together, and which is safe under a
smaller stack by construction because such a routine takes less when there is
less. The word itself holds `0x0040C602`, inside the Mac Plus ROM's address
range.

**What neither instrument can see:** a path no fixture took. SAMPLE is blind
between its sampling points — Standard File's own modal loop has no filter of
ours in it, and the File Manager's frames during `FSWrite` are below
`sink_write`'s sample. So this is read WITH the static table above, never
instead of it, and the 3.8x margin is what covers the gap.

**Three earlier metrics were wrong, and the ways they failed transfer.**
Scanning up from ApplLimit for the first dirty long, and down from the stack
base for the first clean one, both gave the same answer for a 3 KB BinHex
file and a 34-entry StuffIt 5 archive — because a frame is made by MOVING the
stack pointer, so a frame holding an uninitialised buffer leaves clean longs
inside the region it used and dirty longs sit below anything else that writes
there. A third took the lowest dirty long in the top quarter and saturated at
the quarter boundary as soon as the stack was made smaller than four times
the depth. **A single transition finds a hole, not a boundary**, and a metric
with a threshold baked into it reports the threshold.

The deepest chain the sixth join scheme adds is **2,796 B**, measured
the same way on 2026-08-29 and second only to the DiskDoubler one:
`unc_extract_segments` → `unc_mime_extract` → `mime_emit` →
`mime_dec_run` → `mime_dec_next` → `seg_read` → `seg_open` →
`unc_mimeseg_head` → `mseg_scan` (1,256 B of it, a 512-byte read window
and a 512-byte unfolded header) → `unc_read_at`. A joiner that calls a
format reader that reads back through the joiner is the only chain here
that goes through the engine twice, which is why it was measured rather
than assumed.

That 3,290 B became 3,330 B on 2026-08-27: `dd_fork` gained 44 bytes to the
shared fork writer and gave 4 back when its prologue was hoisted out of nine
branches (DECISIONS.md, *the fork writer and the filter*, and *the shapes that
were left*). It is **3,386 B** since later the same day: `UncPath` keeps the
uncut path length per level, which is 56 bytes in `unc_dd_extract` and 52 in
each of the other two walk frames (DECISIONS.md, *the path builder's own
cap*).

What fixed it: DiskDoubler's three `UncCanon` and Now Compress's two moved to
the heap (~3.9 KB each, per fork and per archive respectively), and the zip
end-of-central-directory scan window stopped at 1 KB rather than the 4 KB that
would have made it the deepest frame in the engine on its own.

for a format that reached the table. Method 13 on

(DECISIONS.md, *the fast table StuffIt could not reach*).

**And the same table over ARC's own trees took another 5.31 % off**, the
same day. Squeeze and Distilled store an explicit tree of node pairs and
walked it a bit at a time — 7.0 bits a symbol, of which 98.2 % are ten or
fewer. `derived-INTRECHO.pak`, the largest single run in the corpus:
146,326,346 → **89,159,573**, −39.1 %; `synth-arc-mixed.arc` −3.9 %. The
LZW methods are unmoved, having no tree to walk. ARC's peak allocation
ceiling is **unchanged** at 46,686 B — re-measured across every `.arc`
and `.pak` fixture, and set by one of those LZW methods (DECISIONS.md,
*the tree that was walked a bit at a time*).

**And a one-line third.** `now_bit_offset` sits in the condition of both
Now Compress decode loops, so it ran once per symbol — two million times
on `derived-SyntheticReferences2.0.sea` — and computed an absolute bit
position through `unc_bufin_tell`. A `NowBits` is always memory-backed,
where that call reduces to `pos`: **−2.33 %** on that fixture, −0.7 % on
`synth-now-mixed.now`.

**Where that leaves the three tiers.** Over the same 48 fixtures,
1,098,529,621 Ir before the harmonisation and **1,011,192,657** after all
of it: **−7.95 %**.

**The three envelope rows are the same change and it is not a decoder at all:**
`unc_macbinary_probe` moved ahead of the 64 KB backwards scan that
`unc_zipseg_probe` runs on every file, and the scan itself became a pointer
walk. MacBinary is 234 of the 929 fixtures.

**A real zip is unchanged**, which is the control: `4acalc.zip` is 8 KB, it
finds its end-of-central-directory record in the first window and always
did, and nothing in it is hot enough to move.

**The three CRC functions are four bytes per loop test, walked with a
pointer.** They are the most shared loop in the engine — every format's fork
CRC comes through one of them — and `unc_crc32` alone was **40.7 %** of a
gzip extraction, where zlib does the inflating and this does everything
else. That is what moves gzip and zip off the flat line they sat on through
every earlier change, and it is 4 % on the Huffman formats besides. **Not**
slicing-by-4, which is the usual next step: that wants aligned 32-bit reads
of the input, which the 68000 discipline forbids, and a table four times the
size. Same bytes in, same table, same answer, 384 B of code.

**Both Huffman decoders now peek eight bits and look the code up**, and they
are separate decoders: `unc_canon_decode` reads canonical code lengths
(Compact Pro, DiskDoubler DDn, Now Compress, StuffIt 13, LZH) and
`unc_huff_decode` walks a serialized TREE (StuffIt method 3, DiskDoubler
method 4, PackIt). The second was found by re-profiling after the first
landed — it was 48 % of `derived-EmpireBuilderDemo.sit` and the original
plan had not named it. Its table stores the symbol, because an arbitrary
tree has no arithmetic that recovers one; the canonical table does not need
to.

`unc_bitin_bits` takes its n bits in one masked shift when the window holds
them, instead of n calls to the single-bit reader. That is another 6 to 10
points on DiskDoubler and Now Compress on top of the table.

**Both tables are TEN bits wide, and that was measured rather than
assumed.** Eight is the textbook figure and covers every code of eight bits
or fewer, which for a 256-symbol alphabet is most of the symbols decoded.
Now Compress codes 0x122 symbols and DiskDoubler DDn 257, and there the
tail past eight bits was where the time still was: 8 → 10 is a further
**17.0 %** of a Now Compress run, 8.0 % of DiskDoubler, 7.8 % of Compact
Pro and 8.7 % of the tree decoder's StuffIt fixture. 11 adds 2.7 % more and
12 a further 0.7 %, for two and four times the table, so 10 is the knee.
The cost is 1 KB (canonical) or 2 KB (tree) per table and `1 << K` writes
per build; archives of many small entries were checked for that build cost
and `derived-keyAhead.cpt` got *faster*.

### What is still hot, measured on the same runs

`ars_next_symbol`'s linear symbol search averages **10.4 iterations per
decoded symbol** (471,500 symbol decodes on the ISBN fixture; the loop's two
lines are 4.52 % + 5.37 % of the run against 0.72 % for both divisions). That
kills the obvious idea of replacing `code / renorm` with a multiply per
iteration — ten `__mulsi3` calls would cost a 68000 more than one
`__udivsi3`. The divisions are now unsigned, which deletes `__divsi3`'s sign
wrapper; gcc had **already** merged the third one the plan expected to find.

**BinHex is the one left with an obvious shape and no obvious fix.**
`bh_get_bits` is still 38 % of a large `.hqx` — 600,000 calls at about
sixteen instructions each, over half of them call overhead — and giving it
an inline fast path with the rare cases behind a call measured **worse**
(−5.5 % against −6.6 % without it), for the same reason the bit reader did:
the opaque call forces the cursor fields back to memory. What is left is
decoding four groups to three bytes in a batch, which changes what a stream
truncated mid-triple does, and that is graded by 9,320 robustness variants
rather than argued.

`-flto` is not available: it crashes Retro68's gcc 16.1.0 with an internal
compiler error (`write_symbol, at lto-streamer-out.cc:3097`, during IPA pass
`modref`) on `mac/macextract_mac.c`. Tried 2026-08-24, clear pass/fail, do not
re-try without a newer toolchain.

for 512 bytes of heap and no code at all.

bogus FAIL after about five seconds, with a nonsense value in the grid's `ret`

The alive-grid-read-mid-paint explanation is a **hypothesis**; the pattern is

**Three bytes per sample buys 25 % more density at the same budget**, and on
Linux that moves `derived-Library_ISBN_Barcoder.sit` from `target` 512 to 1024

found the budget error in *Budget* below: the machine's is 597,036 B, not the
678,392 B every simulation here was using.

pair supersedes an earlier one (537 s / 927 s) taken before the progress
rewrite; DECISIONS.md 2026-08-07 records the ~40 s between those two sets as
unexplained.

**8/8, and the shape is the useful part: for small archives the BOOT dominates
and extraction is seconds.** A stripped System 6.0.8 reaches the application in
about 10 s; everything in this table then extracts in under five. The one
larger 1x measurement that exists — `derived-EmpireBuilderDemo.sit`, 278 KB in
and 417 KB out — took **~134 s**. So a few kilobytes is instant, a quarter of a
megabyte is a couple of minutes.

**The large BinHex is not an anomaly.** `macburger-helper.hqx` simply takes
150 s; the rate is very non-uniform (16% of the input in the first seven
minutes, the last 205 KB in a hundred seconds), which is what made the

## What -O3 is worth, and where

Two measurements, 2026-08-30, both on `derived-EmpireBuilderDemo.sit` at
authentic Mac Plus speed (`run.py --emu mv-4M-1x -D UNC_AT_TIMING`, which
reports the extraction in TICKS rather than through a screenshot poll).

**Where an extraction's seconds go.** The probe bills File Manager time by
kind and leaves the engine as the remainder:

| phase | ticks | seconds | share |
|---|---|---|---|
| File Manager reads | 34 | 0.6 s | 0.8 % |
| File Manager writes | 16 | 0.3 s | 0.4 % |
| create, `SetEOF`, close, `FlushVol`, catalog | 12 | 0.2 s | 0.3 % |
| **the engine** | 4,164 | **69.4 s** | **98.5 %** |

So the streaming writer's 4 KB pieces, the `SetFPos` before every read and the
`FlushVol` after every fork cost **1.1 s between them** on this machine. Any
plan that trades one of those for speed is trading a rounding error.

**What the optimiser is worth on the four files that matter**, and why the
answer is a list of functions rather than a flag:

| build | extract | application |
|---|---|---|
| `-Os` everywhere | 70.4 s | 182,144 B |
| `-O2`, `unc_lzw.c` + `unc_huffman.c` | 58.8 s | +1,536 B |
| `-O2`, those plus `unc_util.c` + `fmt_sit.c` | 56.2 s | +5,504 B |
| `-O2`, the whole engine bar `sit_arsenic.c` | 56.0 s | +40,192 B |
| `-O3`, the same four files | 52.5 s | +8,320 B |
| **`UNC_HOT` — six functions, `-O3`** | **52.6 s** | **+1,024 B** |

The last two rows are the point. `-O3` on whole files spent 8,850 B of text,
the largest single item being `unc_huff_read_tree` at +2,428 B — a function
that is 0.44 % of the run. `UNC_HOT` (`src/unc_internal.h`) marks
`unc_lzw_read`, `lzw_code`, `lzw_chain`, `unc_huff_decode`,
`unc_canon_decode`, `unc_crc16_arc` and StuffIt's two hot method decoders,
and gets the same time for an eighth of the size. `unc_sit_decode_fork` came
out 226 B SMALLER than at `-Os`, because GCC will not inline across differing
optimisation attributes and the two marked decoders stopped being copies
inside it.

Verified byte-exact on both Mini vMac tiers with `UNC_HOT` in: **69/69 at
4 MB and 69/69 at the 1 MB floor** (2026-08-30).

### 512 KB machines: the RAM is enough, the ROM is not

**A Macintosh 512Ke — 512 KB, the Plus's 128K ROM — runs it: 66/69 under
System 3.0** (**2026-08-31**, `sys30.py --emu mv-512Ke`; 65/69 before the
reserve went flat, 64/69 before the LZW bound below recovered

must, per entry and not per archive: `derived-Library_ISBN_Barcoder.sit`
writes 33 of its 34 entries with one entry errored, and

measured one, taken before the stack changed). **The budget is 163,656 B** of a 218,208 B free heap,

`synth-dd-m5.dd` is the clean illustration: DiskDoubler method 5 asks for one

**66/69 is the ceiling, and it is a property of the three archives.**
Measured 2026-09-01 with `tools/run_tiers.py`, which applies the same rule on

### A true Macintosh 512K: the machine boots, the application bombs

**A 64 KB ROM cannot read HFS, and that was the whole of "cannot be reached at
all"** — a claim withdrawn 2026-08-31 (BLOCKERS.md) after the one A/B nobody
had run. On HFS the machine dies at startup, **Sad Mac `0F0064`**, with
Apple's 128K ROM (`28BA61CE`) and the 512K's own `28BA4E50` alike, so it is
neither the RAM nor the ROM revision; a blank 800 KB HFS volume with no boot
blocks leaves the frame byte-identical to the no-disk one, so the drive reads
800K and declines to boot it rather than dying on it, and what fails is the
boot code. On **MFS** the same machine boots normally:

| volume | result on `mv-512K` (64 KB ROM, 512 KB of RAM) |
|---|---|
| `Sys30.img` — Apple's 800K HFS System 3.0 floppy | Sad Mac `0F0064` |
| `Sys11.img` — Apple's 400K MFS System 1.1 floppy | boots to Finder 1.1 |
| `Sys11clone.img` — the same two files, volume rebuilt by `mkmfs.py` | boots to Finder 1.1 |
| `Sys30Boot.img` — System 3.0 + Finder 5.1 lifted out of `Sys30.img` onto MFS, with `Sys30.img`'s own boot blocks | boots the floor system |
| `Sys30MFS.img` — **the 400K MFS floppy Apple shipped System 3.0 on**, volume "System Software" | **boots the floor system**, and is what `setup.sh` fetches |

Apple's own MFS disk is the one to use, and `setup.sh` gets it by unpacking
Macintosh Garden's `System_3.x.sit` with this repository's own CLI. Its System
file is **140,299 B against the 800K HFS disk's 181,913 B** — the same version,
41,614 B smaller, because the MFS build carries no HFS.

**The application launches there, and bombs: `ID = 03`.** Three
configurations, one result: double-clicked from an MFS volume under System
1.1, double-clicked under System 3.0, and launched as the boot shell — named
`Finder` — off Apple's own 400K System 3.0 disk, with no Finder on the volume
at all. The Finder mounts the application's volume and draws its icon; the
bomb box comes within seconds of the launch. The System is not the variable
and the machine is.

**What bombs is `MaxApplZone`, the application's second Toolbox call.**
`tools/vmac/traptest` paints a mark, makes one call, paints the next mark, and
never returns; its calls are in the order the application makes them, so where
the probe stops is where the application stops. Both columns are one disk —
System 3.0 and the probe, the probe named `Finder` so the System launches it —
on the two machines that differ in nothing but their ROM:

| call, in the application's order | 512Ke, 128 KB ROM | 512K, 64 KB ROM |
|---|---|---|
| `SetApplLimit(ApplLimit − kStackBump)` | returns | returns |
| `MaxApplZone()` | returns | **bomb** |
| `NGetTrapAddress` (what `TrapAvailable` uses), twice | returns | returns, and answers correctly |
| `PBHGetVInfoSync` — an HFS parameter block | returns | returns |
| `PBGetCatInfoSync` | returns | **bomb, `ID = 12`, unimplemented core routine** |

The 512Ke column is the control, and it is the reason the 512K column means
anything: a probe that stops early proves nothing until the same probe, on the
same volume, has been seen to finish. The last three rows are reachable on a
64K ROM only with `-DTRAPTEST_NO_MEM`, which drops the first two.

**`MaxApplZone` is `[Not in ROM]`** — Inside Macintosh II-30, whose
assembly-language note says to call the Pascal procedure because there is no
trap for it. It became one with the 128K ROM, and Retro68 emits that trap
inline and unconditionally: `pascal void MaxApplZone() M68K_INLINE(0xA063)`,
`Multiverse.h`. **That is not a Retro68 defect and there is nothing to report
upstream** — Retro68 targets System 6, which means a 128K ROM, where the trap
exists; the glue Apple's 1985 interfaces carried for `[Not in ROM]` routines
is absent because nothing Retro68 targets needs it. Writing that glue would
also gain nothing here: the next wall, `PBGetCatInfo`, is not glue but HFS
itself. The same bisect through the real application, marks written
straight into screen memory, stops in the same place: `SetApplLimit` returns,
`MaxApplZone` does not.

**The bomb's ID is not evidence of anything here.** The same call reports
`ID = 03` (illegal instruction), `ID = 10` (line 1111) or `ID = 28` (the stack
has expanded into the heap) depending on the build — Apple's table is Inside
Macintosh II-365 — because what a missing trap dispatches into is whatever
happens to be at that address. The call is constant; the symptom is not.

So neither of the reasons this file used to give was the reason: `TrapAvailable`
answers correctly on a 64K ROM (`_WaitNextEvent` compares equal to
`_Unimplemented`, so the application never calls it), and an HFS parameter
block returns. Behind `MaxApplZone` there is a second wall, `PBGetCatInfo`'s
`_HFSDispatch` selector, which this application uses in five places to walk a
directory and to size a file. The verdict is unchanged and now has a
reproduction: **a 64K-ROM Macintosh cannot run this.**

**One guard makes it start on a 64K ROM, and that guard lives on the branch
`rom64k`, not here** — nothing has run it on the machine it is for, so it is
not in this repository's download (`BRANCH_NOTES.md` on that branch).
`MaxApplZone` is guarded by `Has128KROM()`, two byte reads of the ROM
version word at `ROMBase+8` — Inside Macintosh II's `Environs` note for where
it lives, Inside Macintosh IV's preface for the threshold, `>= 117 ($75)` — so
the call is made on every machine that has it and skipped on the one that does
not. `TrapAvailable` cannot do this job: it reports `_MaxApplZone` as PRESENT
on a 64K ROM, because it compares against `_Unimplemented` and that entry is
neither.

Measured on the emulated 512K under Apple's MFS System 3.0, with that
branch's `dist/`: Standard File opens, a file is chosen, the destination
dialog appears, and a non-archive is opened, sniffed and refused with the
application's own alert, *"This file could not be extracted."* **Extracting a
real StuffIt archive still bombs `ID = 12`** at an HFS call, because this
machine has no HFS.

So a 64K-ROM Macintosh is now **one call short, and that call is not ours**:
HFS is what Apple's `Hard Disk 20` file supplies, on exactly the machines that
have a hard disk. Whether that closes it is **untested and cannot be tested
here** — Mini vMac has no HD20 (MANUAL_TESTS.md §7). What is measured is that
the application no longer dies on arrival.

**HFS in software is real, and it is the whole File Manager.** Inside
Macintosh IV, *The Floppy Disk Interface*: "The external double-sided drive
can be attached to a Macintosh 512K through the back of a Hard Disk 20. The
Hard Disk 20 start-up software contains a device driver for this drive and the
hierarchical (128K ROM) version of the File Manager." So on a 512K with an
HD20, HFS is present — and an 800K drive is a supported medium there, through
the HD20 — which is exactly the configuration MANUAL_TESTS.md §7 asks about.

**Without that software there is no HFS at all**, measured twice: under
Apple's 400K MFS System 3.0 (a 140,299 B System) and under the 182 KB System
3.0 lifted off the 800K HFS disk, an HFS floppy in the second drive gets
*"This is not a Macintosh disk: Do you want to initialize it?"* The extra
41,614 B of the HFS-disk System is not hierarchical File Manager for a 64K
ROM.

**And the software cannot be loaded here.** The `Hard Disk 20` file is what
hangs, not Apple's disk: a boot disk built here carrying System 3.0, Finder
5.1 and that one file stops at the same empty dialog frame as Apple's own *HD
20 Startup* disk, and stays there — frames byte-identical from 45 s to 210 s,
so it is a deadlock and not a timeout. *Hypothesis:* the driver is hunting for
hardware Mini vMac does not emulate.
HFS was not only a ROM feature: Apple shipped it for 64K-ROM machines as a
file, `Hard Disk 20`, on the *HD 20 Startup* disk — a 400K MFS floppy that
comes in the same `System_3.x.sit` as `Sys30MFS.img`. That disk boots to the
Finder on the 512Ke and stops on the 512K at an **empty dialog frame that
neither Return nor a click dismisses**, unmodified and rebuilt alike.
*Hypothesis, not measured:* Mini vMac's 128K model does not provide the Hard
Disk 20 hardware the driver goes looking for. It would not change the verdict
in any case: `MaxApplZone` is a Memory Manager trap and bombs before any file
call, so the two walls are independent and the one HFS could remove is the
second.

Two harness facts fell out of it. **System 3.0 ignores the boot blocks' shell
name on MFS exactly as it does on HFS** (*System 3.0 tier*), so the probe is
launched by being NAMED `Finder` — `mkmfs.py ... probe.bin:Finder` — which is
deterministic where a double-click is not. **System 1.1 does honour that
field**, measured with `mkmfs.py --shell TrapTest`; it is the only system here
that does.

**One thing behaves differently, and it is the System rather than this
application: Cmd-. does not cancel Standard File on System 3.0.** The frame
after the chord is the Open dialog unchanged, byte-identical to the frame
before it. The chord is arriving — the same chord, in the same run, cancels an
extraction through this application's own progress dialog — so old Standard
File simply does not act on it. The Cancel BUTTON quits normally: the frame
after it is byte-identical to the Finder window the application was launched
from. On System 6.0.8 the same chord closes the same dialog.

`os9.py --encrypted` is the one subject this tier cannot take from `fixtures/`:

### SpaceSaver on Mac OS 9, and the extension the shell was eating (2026-08-28)

The one format in the table that had never been replayed on a Macintosh
harness, because its only specimens are the ones Aladdin's own modules wrote
under `qemu-m68k` and those cannot be committed. They can be RUN, by path:

    os9.py ~/.cache/macextract/vmac/specimens/spacesaver/modules/m4.ss --launch

| | measured |
|---|---|
| subject | `m4.ss`, 6,656 B — method 4, the preset-dictionary one |
| output | **76,800 B byte-exact** against `macextract x`, one boot |
| the name | the Mac writes **`archive.sit~1`**: SpaceSaver compressed a file in place, so the output is named after the archive, and the archive is still sitting in the destination folder |

**The first run of it found a defect, in the shell rather than the engine.**
`cli/main.c` has excepted SpaceSaver from the "hint is the archive name minus
its last extension" rule since the format went in — it compresses in place and
adds no extension, so the whole name is the name — and

therefore came out of the Macintosh application as `Report`, and as
`Report.txt` everywhere else. Fixed the same day, and this tier is now the
regression test: a shell that strips again fails as one MISSING and one
UNEXPECTED name (DECISIONS.md 2026-08-28, *the exception one front end had*).

### Onto HFS+ (the same machine, the boot volume instead of the transfer disk)

Everything above extracts onto the transfer volume, which is HFS. The OS 9
boot volume is HFS+, so `--hfsplus` renames it to `Out` from the keyboard and
runs the **autotest** build against it: the extraction lands on HFS+, and the
application CRC-32s both forks after reading them back through the File
Manager, exactly as the Mini vMac tiers do.

    tools/vmac/os9.py fixtures/derived-Berkeley_Packet_Monitor.sit \
                      -o <dir> --hfsplus

| fixture | files | data | rsrc | peak heap | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `derived-Berkeley_Packet_Monitor.sit` | 31 | 372,301 B | — | 779.1 KB | ✅ CRC + length identical to Linux |
| `derived-XferIt_1.5b4.sea` | 10 | 679,423 B | 268,752 B | 18.8 KB | ✅ CRC + length identical to Linux |

Both report `ret=0` with zero per-entry errors, and every output **name** on
HFS+ is exactly what the 31-character rule predicts — including the one entry
in the Berkeley fixture whose name is 34 characters and must be truncated. A
68k application reaching HFS+ through the classic File Manager is capped at 31
characters just as it is on HFS, so HFS+ changes nothing here; that is the
result, and it was worth measuring rather than assuming.

Two things make this tier trustworthy:

- **The answer comes back as a file, not off the screen** — `at_report_file()`
  on the plain-HFS input volume, because the framebuffer channel that works on
  a Mac Plus is invisible on OS 9's 32-bit screen. The output volume was never
  the obstacle; the reporting channel was (DECISIONS.md 2026-08-02).
- **That the output really landed on HFS+ is measured.** hfsutils sees only the
  HFS wrapper, and after the run it still contains exactly one file,
  `Where_have_all_my_files_gone?` — so none of those 41 files went to plain HFS.
  A signature word proves nothing here (BLOCKERS.md).

One corroboration falls out of it: the Mac's **unmasked** resource-fork CRC
equals Linux's **masked** one, so Mac OS 9 leaves the 112-byte reserved header
area alone where System 6 stamps it — the same thing the byte-level comparison
found on the HFS tier.

### The `SIZE` partition under OS 9's Process Manager

Built with a 600 KB preferred partition and run against
`derived-KeyGraphics.sit`, which needs ~656 KB: **1 entry refused, 0 files,
`ret == UNC_OK`, peak heap 35.5 KB** — the same figure System 7.0.1 gives, on
a machine with 512 MB of RAM. The partition governs, not the hardware, and
35.5 KB is parser state alone: the 512 KB Arsenic block was declined at
request time, never allocated.

Same binary, same fixture, three systems — the partition governs and System 6
without MultiFinder ignores `SIZE` outright, exactly as the resource's comment
always claimed and nothing had ever shown:

| System | Outcome | Engine peak heap |
|---|---|---|
| System 7.0.1 (4 MB) | **1 entry refused, 0 files** | 35.5 KB |
| Mac OS 9.2 (512 MB) | **1 entry refused, 0 files** | 35.5 KB |
| System 6.0.8 (4 MB) | 1 file, byte-exact | 656.8 KB |

The 656.8 KB row is the control — the same figure the 4 MB sweep records for
that fixture, with nothing changed but the partition. Method and commands in
`tools/vmac/README.md` ("The `SIZE` partition").

**Still not covered here:** names HFS+ permits that HFS does not — probably
unreachable through the classic File Manager at all, since it caps at 31
characters on either filesystem, but that is unproven either way. Real PowerPC
silicon, like real 68k silicon, remains untouched.

## Compared with StuffIt Expander 4.0.2 (Aladdin, April 1996)

The free tool a user would otherwise run on the machine itself, and the only
external yardstick this project has (why it was worth applying: DECISIONS.md
2026-08-15). **The coverage and capability tables below are its documentation
against our measurements** — no row in either is a head-to-head observation of
its behaviour. The TIMES are: since 2026-08-29 both applications run on the
same emulated Mac Plus, over the same archive, timed by one instrument
(*Timed on the same Mac Plus*, below).

**Where the Expander column comes from.** Its own distribution,
`stuffit_expander_4.0.2.sea_.bin` from Macintosh Garden: MacBinary around a
StuffIt self-extracting archive around a 400K HFS image. `macextract x` walked
the chain and produced an image byte-identical to `unar`'s (410,624 B, md5
`888c0ca0b93f6ee0ff67e89926e20f83`), and every quotation below is a string
read out of that image — the shipped Read Me, the "What's new" file and the
application's own alert strings. Web sources are used for dates and for what
later versions did, never for what 4.0.2 does.

### Timed on the same Mac Plus (2026-08-29, one row re-run 2026-08-30)

Both applications booted on Mini vMac `mv-4M-1x` — authentic 7 MHz, 4 MB,
System 6.0.8 — over the same archive on the same volume, driven to a Standard
File dialog and timed from the action that starts the work to the last frame
that changed. `tools/vmac/expander.py --quiet 60`; the instrument and what it
cannot see are in `tools/vmac/README.md`.

| Fixture | Format | In | MacExtract | Expander 4.0.2 | Expander ÷ ours |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `derived-Export.sit` | StuffIt | 1.6 KB | 3 s | 3 s | both at the poll floor |
| `derived-EmpireBuilderDemo.sit` | StuffIt | 278 KB | 56 s | 56 s | **1.00x** |
| `derived-FirstClass_Demo.cpt` | Compact Pro | 249 KB | **133 s** | 156 s | **1.18x** |
| `macburger-helper.hqx` | BinHex → StuffIt | 307 KB | **269 s** | 426 s | **1.58x** |

**Neither is faster.** The two are level on StuffIt — 56 s each, which at a
±3 s poll means indistinguishable — and we expand a Compact Pro archive in five
sixths of Expander's time and a BinHex envelope with an archive inside it in
under two thirds. On this evidence a user choosing between them on speed alone
has nothing to choose by.

**That row was 76 s against 56 s on 2026-08-29**, and 73 s after the LZW
change; what closed it was `UNC_HOT`, below. The Compact Pro and BinHex rows
predate it and are therefore pessimistic about us by some unmeasured amount —
both share `unc_canon_decode` and `unc_crc16_arc` with the row that moved.

**Every figure is a lower bound with a ±3 s poll error, and the RATIO is what
transfers**: both sides pay the same quantisation and the same stolen CPU, and
neither absolute column belongs beside *Measured on a 7 MHz Mac Plus* above —
that table is the autotest build, which calls `RunExtract` once and CRC-reads
both forks back off the volume, so it measures neither the same layers nor the
same work. `macburger-helper.hqx` is the clearest case: 104 s there is the
BinHex decode alone, 269 s here is that plus the 312-file archive inside it.

**They wrote the same names.** On every fixture the two output sets are
identical, which is what makes the times comparable at all — two applications
that did different amounts of work would not be. Three differences, all of
them each application's own default and none of them affecting the above:
Expander gathers a multi-item archive into a `<archive> Folder` where we
extract beside the archive; Expander writes a preferences file into the System
Folder; and on the chain **Expander leaves the intermediate
`MacburgerHelper.sit` on the volume**, where the shell deletes each envelope
once the next layer is out.

**Our figures include a `FlushVol` after every fork and Expander's include
none.** That is not an instrument artefact, it is the two designs. Both halves
were measured on 2026-08-30 by reading the image the emulator is writing,
rather than inferred from an empty volume after a kill: when the screen stops
changing our three files are on the volume and Expander's are **not there at
all**, and its four appear when it is sent Command-Q — which commits them
**within a second**, without repainting anything.

**So the asymmetry is real and it is not the explanation.** Under a second of
415 KB does not close a 16 s gap. Read it as a fact about this emulator, whose
disk is a host file with no seek time; a real Mac Plus committing that much to
a floppy or a SCSI disk would not be free, and nothing here can measure that
(MANUAL_TESTS.md).

**The StuffIt row was re-run on 2026-08-30** and moved 76 s → 73 s, with
Expander's 56 s → 57 s and the ratio 0.74x → 0.77x. Our column is consistent
with the LZW change landed that day (−5.3 % on this archive, *Decode cost*
above) but 3 s is one poll interval, so it is consistent-with and not measured.
The other three rows are unaffected by that change — 0.00 % on Compact Pro and
on `macburger-helper.hqx` — and stand as taken.
