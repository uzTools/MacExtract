# MacExtract

MacExtract opens old Macintosh archives and gives back the files inside
them as they were: both forks, type and creator, dates, and each icon in
its place, so an extracted application simply runs. It runs on every
Macintosh from System 3.0 to Mac OS 9, in 512K or more, with nothing else
to install.

## USING IT

MacExtract runs from this disk as it is. To keep it, drag it to your hard
{HFS}disk, with the MacExtract Helpers folder.
{MFS}disk.

To open an archive, double-click it, or open MacExtract and choose Open
from the File menu. MacExtract then asks where the files should go. That
window opens in the archive's own folder, so Extract puts them next to
it; Drive (Desktop on System 7) goes to another disk, and New makes a
folder. Files can be written to a hard disk or an 800K disk, not to a
400K one.

{HFS}With no hard disk, open MacExtract from this disk and choose Open from
{HFS}the File menu. Click Eject, put in the disk with your archive and
{HFS}choose it. The Mac asks for each disk in turn.

When it has finished, anything that had to change is listed: a name that
was too long, or a file that could not be extracted. Save as Text keeps
the list. MacExtract quits after each archive; open it again for the
next one, or select several archives in the Finder and open them
together.

If an archive has a password, MacExtract asks for it, and asks again if
it is wrong.

For an archive in several parts, open the first part. The others are
found in the same folder, or asked for: put in the disk that holds the
part. Parts named .001, .002 or .z01, .z02 are found by their names, so
keep the names they came with. Extract onto a different disk from the
parts.

## THE MACEXTRACT HELPERS

Each helper stands in for a program that made archives, such as StuffIt,
Compact Pro or DiskDoubler, so that double-clicking one of its archives
opens it in MacExtract.
{HFS}Keep the MacExtract Helpers folder beside MacExtract. Once both are
{HFS}on your hard disk, open the folder once so the Finder sees the helpers.
{MFS}They are in MacExtract-Helpers.zip: open it with MacExtract and
{MFS}extract it to your hard disk, beside MacExtract. That gives you the
{MFS}MacExtract Helpers folder; open it once so the Finder sees the helpers.

## CURRENT LIMITATIONS

On System 6 and 3.0, a program installed after the helpers shows the
generic application icon instead of its own.

With one floppy drive, extracting from one floppy onto another swaps the
two disks every few KB. Extract onto a hard disk if you have one.

## WHAT IT OPENS

- StuffIt (.sit), classic and 5.x, StuffIt SpaceSaver, and self-extracting archives (.sea)
- Compact Pro (.cpt)
- BinHex (.hqx), and the older .hex, .hcx and SUMacC .dl
- MacBinary (.bin), AppleSingle and AppleDouble
- PackIt (.pit)
- DiskDoubler and MacCompress
- Now Compress
- Alysis SuperDisk! (.X)
- AppleLink packages (.pkg)
- Private File (.pf)
- Zip (.zip), gzip (.gz), tar (.tar) and Unix compress (.Z)
- ARC (.arc) and LZH/LHA (.lzh, .lha)
- uuencode (.uu), btoa, base64 and mail with attachments (MIME)
- MAR (.mar, .mac) and LZ4 (.lz4)
- Apple Backup disks, even with some missing
- the Tomes on Apple's install disks, and the files Apple's Installer keeps compressed
- archives split into parts by StuffIt, Compact Pro or DiskDoubler, spanned zips (.z01), pieces named .001, and mail sent in several messages

Archives inside archives, such as .sit.hqx or .tar.Z, come out in one go.
Anything else, StuffIt X, RAR and 7-Zip among them, is not opened:
MacExtract says "Unknown format."
